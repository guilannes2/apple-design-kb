### What the images show
Basis: 26 of 26 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.
- The history of notifications is told with real screens in sequence, not with text: a blue modal alert centered over the original iPhone's app grid, a banner at the top of the iPhone 4's locked screen, a list of three stacked notifications and, finally, a calendar invite card that expands with three stacked full-width buttons, Accept, Maybe and Decline (sheet 0002, q0010 to q0018).
- The quick-adjustment menu appears in increasing stages of detail: first just the overlaid card with the app name at the top and full-width stacked buttons, with solid blue marking the recommended action (sheet 0004, q0033 to q0035); then the complete action sheet in which "Deliver Quietly" fades and a second confirmation layer opens inside the same card, with the red destructive button set apart from the rest (sheet 0013, q0110 to q0113).
- The permission request is shown as a binary choice in the same composition: the traditional modal alert on the left, a card integrated into the Notification Center on the right, with a thinking-face emoji appearing between the two and then the modal disappearing, leaving only the new route (sheet 0007, q0061 to q0063); the same side-by-side pair returns on sheet 0009 (q0077 and q0078).
- The new prompt extends the notification's own platter downward, with the "Keep" and "Turn Off" buttons inside the Notification Center card, reusing the modal's side-by-side button pair without opening any modal (sheet 0006, q0053 and q0054).
- A four-item agenda list (First run, Provide value, Notification Grouping, Richer Rich Notifications) reappears throughout the whole talk with the current item in full white and the rest in faded gray, sometimes with a small device icon beside it (sheet 0005, q0042 to q0045; sheet 0009, q0080; sheet 0014, q0120; sheet 0016, q0144). The notes record that the speech doesn't verbalize this feature at every change.
- Short phrases in large white typography on a black background work as dividers between blocks, almost always with no product image alongside (sheets 0003, 0008, 0009, 0010 and 0011, among them "Attention is valuable." and "Considered delivery.", q0021, q0023, q0069 to q0071, q0081 to q0084, q0086 and q0095). The exception is q0066, in which the phrase divides the screen with a small white iPhone icon on the left.
- Real notifications from third-party apps come in as concrete proof, always in the system's standard platter, a colored square icon on the left, the app name in caps with "Just now", a bold title and one line of body text, in translucent red and pink over a teal wallpaper (sheet 0010, q0089 and q0090; sheet 0012, q0101).
- Three different settings screens share the same list component with a toggle on the right, varying only in whether each row gets a colored icon and a description line: the system's generic screen with sections in caps and three alert-type icons, the simple list of alerts for a team, and the list of news sections with icon and subtitle (sheet 0012, q0108; sheet 0013, q0115 to q0117).
- Grouping is demonstrated through the difference between frames: cards accumulating from one to three on the locked screen (sheet 0015, q0127 to q0129), a group header with "Clear" and an arrow appearing at the top of the stack after the tap (sheet 0014, q0125 and q0126), and a card being pulled out of the stack, shifted to the right, to be explained in isolation (sheet 0015, q0134 and q0135; sheet 0016, q0136 to q0138).
- There's a code slide in monospaced font, colored keywords on an almost-black background and comments in gray, showing the creation of the notification content object with the title, body and thread identifier lines, with the presenter semi-transparent behind the block (sheet 0015).
- The anatomy of the expanded rich notification becomes explicit: the Photos card gets a circular "x" in the corner, the photo at full width, the text below and two stacked full-width buttons; the Calendar card swaps the photo for a small grid of times with colored blocks and three stacked buttons (sheet 0017, q0148 to q0152). The notes record that the buttons on these cards are always stacked at full width, never side by side.
- The reaction gesture is shown through three consecutive frames: a horizontal capsule with six reaction icons appearing above the bubble, then the capsule vanishing and a small badge becoming attached to the corner of the bubble, with a full QWERTY keyboard at the bottom (sheet 0018, q0154 to q0156). Alongside it, a podcast notification consolidates three episodes into a list with a full-width button to play all of them (q0158 and q0159).
- On Apple Watch each type of interaction gets its own control inside the notification, over a full-screen black background: a row of five stars plus a payment button, two round minus and plus buttons with the value changing between frames, a circular display with a large number inside a ring and arrows, and a list with round checkboxes plus a highlighted button and a secondary button (sheet 0022, q0194 to q0197; sheet 0023, q0200 to q0206).
- The relationship between the two devices is shown in three ways: an iPhone with a padlock overlaid next to the watch, which keeps showing the alert (sheet 0020, q0179 and q0180), an annotation diagram with lines and labels connecting corresponding elements between the phone version and the watch version in place of real screenshots (sheet 0021, q0188 and q0189), and three iPhone-plus-Watch pairs side by side at the close, with the watch in a more compact version than the phone's (sheet 0026, q0226 to q0229).
- Two graphic devices break the pattern of the cards: a notification card drawn as a flat rectangle outside any device frame, with a color bar at the top, in the format of a spec piece (sheet 0022), and a QR code filling the entire display in white, with no other interface element (sheet 0024, q0214 and q0215).
Visual proportion: practically the whole video has content on screen, iOS and watchOS screenshots, text slides, agenda lists and a code slide, with the opening sheet and a few standalone close-ups as the only stretches with just the presenter (sheets 0001, 0003, 0015, 0016, 0018, 0020, 0024 and 0025).
