### What the images show
Basis: 10 of 10 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.
- The same fictional app screen works as a test bench from beginning to end: title and subtitle inside an iPhone frame at the opening and, in the next two frames, the same composition enlarged with the device frame already removed (sheet 0001, q0006 to q0008) and then isolated next to a feeling question with the font's real name written below, "Sophisticated?" with Cochin and Snell Roundhand, "Literary?" with Big Caslon and Adobe Caslon Pro, "Fresh?" with Avenir (sheet 0005, q0040 to q0045; sheet 0006, q0046). The screen records pair by pair which font produces which effect, something the speech treats only as a general impression.
- The practical rules appear in writing on three list slides with the same template, four items for text font (low to moderate contrast, simpler structure, regular to medium weights, comfortable spacing at small size), three items for display font (high contrast allowed, weight different from the text, tighter spacing) and four items for pairing (sheet 0004, q0032 and q0033; sheet 0009, q0080). These slides are the literal and complete record of the guidelines.
- Technical proportion diagram: two lowercase "h"s inside identical rectangular guide boxes, one thin script font and one straight sans serif, and in the next frame a vertical double arrow labeled "Point size" appears, measuring the height of the box (sheet 0007, q0059 to q0060). It is the graphic demonstration that the font's body, not the letter, defines the nominal size.
- Measurement annotations on a real example: the word "Millions" appears repeated on two lines labeled "Display" on top and "Text" below (sheet 0003, q0024 and q0025) and only afterward receives thin red and blue circles over specific letters, which change position between frames, one on the top line and two on the bottom one (q0026 and q0027; sheet 0004, q0028). The speech does not say where the circles are; only the screen locates the contrast points discussed.
- Variable isolated frame by frame: a pair of lowercase "n"s, one cursive with a single stroke and another built with separate strokes, progressively gains weight until it becomes two high-contrast bold serif versions, keeping the same letter structure (sheet 0003, q0019 to q0022).
- Calligraphy vocabulary assembled with tool icons: three symbols of different writing systems, each with a brush, a broad-nib pen or a pointed pen below it, and focus shifting from one to another through brightness and opacity (sheet 0002, q0012 to q0016); then the screen narrows down to the pointed pen with two vertical strokes, one of uniform weight and another modulated, wide in the middle and thin at the tips (q0017 to q0018).
- The same brightness-highlight technique is reused to compare three treatments of the word "Display" in the top row and the same three of "Text" below, alternating which option is lit (sheet 0004, q0029 to q0030).
- Two case studies in product format, not sample text: a dictionary app with a running-text definition and green and blue color highlights over specific terms, including the grammatical category "mass noun" (sheet 0007, q0061 to q0063; sheet 0008, q0065), and a newspaper article layout with a condensed bold title, small date and reading body, shown at various sizes and positions (sheet 0008, q0069 to q0072; sheet 0009, q0073 to q0077).
- Apparent scale compared directly: the word "mass" repeated four times in varied sizes and weights, in two columns, with no explanatory label on the screen (sheet 0008, q0064).
- Reading test in sentence context: two identical lines in English, one above the other, in visibly different typefaces, the top one more condensed and higher contrast, the bottom one more uniform and spaced out (sheet 0003, q0023).
- Typographic humor used as argument: "LTypI" slide with the expansion in small type in the footer, a pun slide about choosing Futura by name, and a fictional event agenda in which each time line uses a different handwritten font (sheet 0007, q0055 to q0058), preceded by the invented identity of a sign painters' conference (sheet 0006, q0053 to q0054).
- The topic list "Functionality / Style / Pairing" is reused as an index, appearing at the opening, resurfacing by dissolve behind the guidelines slide and returning with the third item lit up in white and the other two grayed out (sheet 0002, q0010; sheet 0004, q0033 to q0034; sheet 0006, q0051 and q0052).
- The visual arc closes on the same list of font names from the macOS selector shown at the opening, each name rendered in its own typography, changing only the emoji next to it, from a thinking face to a sunglasses face (sheet 0001, q0005; sheet 0009, q0081). The speech does not mention this change.
Visual proportion: almost the entire video is a teaching slide or app mockup on screen, with the presenter alone in frame in a few transition moments (sheet 0001 q0009, sheet 0004 q0031 and q0035, sheet 0006 q0047 to q0050, sheet 0008 q0066 to q0068, sheet 0009 q0078 and q0079).
Recorded divergences or limits: sheet 0010 has a single frame (q0082) with only the presenter, and the rest of the grid is empty because it is the end of the video.
