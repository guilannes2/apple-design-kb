### What the images show
Basis: 13 of 13 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.
- Practically every content card is built as a list that grows item by item between successive frames, with the new item in strong white and the rest in gray: it happens in "Switch Control users" (sheet 0002, q0012 to q0015), "Special considerations" (sheet 0004, q0033 to q0035, and sheet 0005, q0037), "Challenges for Switch Control" (sheet 0007, q0056 to q0061), "Other useful API" (sheet 0010, q0087 to q0089), "Best practices" (sheet 0011, q0094 to q0097) and "Wrap up" (sheet 0012, q0105 to q0106).
- The three-line agenda card on a wood background, with a phone alongside, appears twice as the session's progress marker, with the current block in white and the rest in gray: "APIs" on sheet 0005 (q0039) and "Best practices" on sheet 0011 (q0092). On sheet 0001 the transition to the first block uses another device, a black title card with the first topic and the word "APIs" in the corner, which the note also describes as text overlaid on the presenter's image (q0008), with no wood background or the three lines.
- The Switch Control cursor focus has a fixed visual convention in the example app's captures: a solid blue outline rectangle around the element, shifting position between neighboring frames to indicate advancement. It appears on the level map node (sheet 0007, q0056 to q0057), on the card grid (sheet 0008, q0068 to q0069) and in the manual scan of level 6 (sheet 0010, q0084 to q0085). In the city choice list the same blue highlight falls on "Algiers, Algeria" during the autoscan (sheet 0004, q0029 to q0030).
- On sheets 0007 to 0009 each Swift code snippet is paired with the app screen it affects, and the internal highlight changes from frame to frame following the explanation: on sheet 0009 the highlight moves from the whole block to the underlined class name (q0076 to q0077) and then to the action array assignment line, marked with a vertical bar and gray background (q0077 to q0078). On sheet 0010 the block with the two declarations appears next to the "Other useful API" list card, not an app screen, and the second line gets a highlighted background when the second item enters (q0087 to q0089). Without the image there is no way to know which line is in focus at each moment.
- Marking something as new has its own graphic device: a green "NEW" tag next to the line that sets the custom action's image via a system symbol (sheet 0009, q0078 to q0080 and q0081).
- The relationship between hardware and interface is shown literally on one sheet: a hand presses two buttons, one yellow and one red, labeled "Select Item" and "Next Item", next to the running game, and the focus highlight advances across the grid between frames (sheet 0010, q0082 to q0085). In the other appearances the physical switch is a scene object, with no hand recorded in the notes: the round blue button on the presenter's table (sheet 0001, from q0004 on) and the large red switch on a black base next to the city list (sheet 0004, q0029 and q0030).
- The anatomy of the example app's screen is made explicit in the frames: a red top bar for the level with a house icon, a light blue strip with the prompt, a grid of rounded cards with a hand icon for the flipped state, and a color code per state, blue for correct, red for wrong, green for the second piece, a blue diamond with a question mark for marking, plus a centered success modal darkening the background (sheet 0006, q0048 to q0054).
- The accessibility problems are presented as image and highlight pairs, not just as text: the blue rectangle jumps from one map node to another, illustrating the navigation order under the list item about lack of clear grouping (sheet 0007, q0056 to q0057); the illustrated hand over the red-highlighted card disappears in the next frame, marking the before and after of the tap (q0058 to q0059); and the gestures submenu panel expands and comes to occupy more of the game screen, with labeled icons among them "Double Tap" (q0060 to q0061).
- The effect of the fix also appears in the interface itself: next to the custom actions code, the game screen shows a horizontal submenu of four icons with a short caption, "Tap", "Add Card", "Pin Card" and "Tap and Hold", representing Switch Control's top-level menu already with the icons applied (sheet 0009).
- Real system screens come in as evidence, not as illustration: the Settings screen with the automatic card-flip toggle off and then on (sheet 0008, q0065 to q0067), the Reminders swipe actions example with the orange and red buttons revealed (sheet 0008, q0071) and the Switch Control Settings list with "Scanning Style", "Auto Scanning Time" and the other options being tapped by a finger (sheet 0012, q0107).
- The documentary sheet replaces the slide when the subject is the person: a sequence of the user in a motorized wheelchair going down the trail to the waterfall, a close-up of the mouth switch, the iPhone camera viewfinder with the capture modes and, at the end, a credits card in white text on black (sheet 0003, q0019 to q0025).
- Isolated pictograms reinforce a specific list item with no extra text: a circular blue clock icon next to "No timeouts" (sheet 0011, q0097 to q0098) and a shield with a checkmark, partially visible in the background of the final best-practices card (sheet 0012, q0101).
- A single sentence gets its own card, large centered typography on a black background, with no other element, to fix the thesis that good VoiceOver accessibility already delivers almost all the support (sheet 0005, q0040 to q0041).
- The brand frame opens and closes the session in mirrored fashion: a still of the laptop with the stickers and the Memoji alongside at the opening (sheet 0001, q0002 and q0003) and the same still repeated after the event logo at the close (sheet 0013, q0109 to q0111).
Visual proportion: of the 13 sheets, only 0013 is exclusively a brand frame; sheets 0006 to 0012 are dominated by app screens, code blocks and slide cards; 0003 is predominantly documentary footage, with the last frames returning to the presenter; sheets 0001, 0002 and 0005 alternate the presenter at the table with title cards, and 0004 adds an iPhone screen demonstration.
Recorded divergences or limits: the note for sheet 0001 describes the opening block as a black title card with the "APIs" label in the corner, overlaid on the presenter's image, while the synthesis of the notes themselves treats this appearance as the same agenda card from sheets 0005 and 0011; the hand pressing the physical switch is only recorded on sheet 0010, in the others the switch appears as a scene object; the shield with the checkmark on sheet 0012 is only partially visible in the background (q0101).
