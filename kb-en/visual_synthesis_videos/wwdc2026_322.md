### What the images show
Basis: 13 of 13 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.

- The pipeline metaphor is built as an object, not as an abstract diagram: a 3D illustration of metal piping with joints, a glass and chrome flow meter labeled "BYTES" and a red valve (sheet 0001, q0007 and q0008). It returns at the close already labeled in large typography over a light checkered background (sheet 0013, q0109 and q0110) and is reinforced by a physical pipe wrench next to an iPhone on wood in the final slides (sheet 0013, q0112 to q0114).
- The diagrams use a stable notation system throughout the video: a circular lens attached to the pipe equals data state, with a thumbnail of the content at that point; a rectangular box in the middle of the pipe equals an applied transformation; a Y branch equals a branch; an X crossing equals a merge of two flows (sheet 0002, q0014 to q0018; sheet 0003, q0019 to q0025). The same lens vocabulary reaches the result preview, framed by a dark circular vignette that closes in further between q0026 and q0027 (sheet 0003).
- The decomposition of the existing interface appears as a physical branching: a single pipe labeled "Original UI" opens into three outputs with their own preview, colored image, playback symbol and text block (sheet 0002, q0016 to q0018). Between q0016 and q0018 the cover branch's preview stops being a generic rectangle and becomes a gradient image, marking the transformation in progress.
- The names of the pipeline stages exist only on screen: the boxes carry labels like "Shader effects", "Timeline input", "View overlay" and "Auto scroll" (sheet 0003, q0019 to q0024), technical captions that the speech does not say in that passage.
- The dominant teaching pattern is code and result side by side: a block in monospaced font with syntax highlighting (gray comment, red or orange string, purple parameter and types, green function name) next to an iPhone mockup that reflects that code (sheets 0004, 0006, 0007, 0008, 0009 and 0010). In sheets 0006 and 0007 the block becomes a labeled pair, one side SwiftUI and the other Metal; in sheet 0008 the notes record a single Metal block, without the pair, and in sheets 0009 and 0010 the block goes back to being SwiftUI only.
- Before and after the blur, with just one line of difference: q0031 shows the sharp gradient cover image and q0032 shows the same composition visibly blurred after the blur modifier with radius 30 appears in the code (sheet 0004).
- The shader explanation uses a grid of pixels in shades of blue labeled "Input" and "Output", with yellow label boxes annotating values and arrows linking the input pixel to the output pixel (sheet 0005, q0041 to q0045). In the same block, the light blue syntax highlight moves through the three shader signatures one at a time, first, second and third line (sheet 0005, q0041 to q0042; sheet 0006, q0047 to q0048), turning the API list into a reading script.
- The difference in the layer effect is shown by area rather than by text: instead of a single pixel, a larger square delimits a sampled region on the input, linked by an arrow to a specific pixel on the output (sheet 0006, q0049 and q0050).

Between q0064 and q0065 the dot changes position in all three channels, demonstrating the sampling.
