### What the images show
Basis: 17 of 17 frame sheets opened, all codes checked.

- The evolution of the interface is shown with photographed physical objects, not with screen captures: an old beige computer with two drives isolated in the dark, hands typing on an integrated beige keyboard, the phosphor green screen with uppercase commands and a blinking cursor, the original Macintosh with a rectangular mouse and a four-label menu bar, and finally an original iPhone with the "slide to unlock" band (sheet 0001, q0009; sheet 0002, q0010 to q0018; sheet 0003, q0021).
- The talk's structure appears as a recurring list slide, with the current topic in bold black and the rest in light gray, left-aligned in a single column, serving as a map between the blocks. The synthesis of the notes gathers under this device sheet 0001, q0007; sheet 0008, q0068 to q0070; sheet 0014, q0118 and q0119; sheet 0015, q0127 to q0129; and sheet 0016, q0143 and q0144, although the record for the last two describes black-background slides with the title of the current topic and its items. The notes record that the speech does not describe this device.
- The principle lists are built item by item instead of appearing complete: a black slide shows only the title, then gains three lines, then a fourth (sheet 0013, q0114, q0115 and q0116); the same accumulation happens from one to two items and from one to three in other sheets (sheet 0015, q0128 to q0129; sheet 0016, q0143 to q0144).
- The zone and parameter annotations exist only in the image: two luminous concentric rings around the speaker mark the distance zones, markers numbered 1 and 2 are placed over the line between phone and speaker, and a zoom reveals the list of parameters that vary by zone, among them banner position and scale, background blur, haptic strength, device light and audio (sheet 0009, q0078 to q0081). The notes record that the speech describes the two boundaries and the continuous feedback, but not this diagram.
- The scale of the interface element follows proximity: between two product stills of the same composition, the player card on the lock screen goes from a compact band to a larger band with a large album cover, a reading the notes support with the speech about banner position and scale (sheet 0010, q0082 to q0083).
- The same interface space changes function depending on distance, without changing position: the button that brings directions with distance and time becomes the find button when the target is close, keeping the pair of buttons in the same place on the card (sheet 0006, q0048 and q0050 versus q0052).
- The search screen has three distinct visual stages depending on distance: a pattern of dots in a circle with no defined direction, an arrow with the distance in feet over a dark background, and an entirely green screen reduced to a large central white circle with the distance as the only supporting text (sheet 0007, q0055, q0057 and q0062; sheet 0013, q0109 and q0110, where only the distance number changes).
- Background color is the main vehicle for state reinforcement in this family of screens: the structure remains, a large arrow at the center and a distance line below, and what alternates is the background, between neutral dark and solid green, to mark alignment with the right direction (sheet 0012, q0101 to q0102; sheet 0016, q0138 to q0139). In both pairs the direction text changes along with the background, according to the record for each sheet.
- The anatomy of the location screen is fixed and lean: status label at the top, large arrow at the center, a small dotted arc above it, distance and direction in text right below, and only two buttons at the bottom, cancel and play sound, with nothing else (sheet 0011, q0095; sheet 0012, q0107).
- Not everything that looks like a screen is a screen: the notes record that the green light cone coming from the top of the phone toward the sofa is an illustrative graphic overlay, created to communicate the idea of the screen lighting up, and not a literal capture (sheet 0007, q0060).
- The negative example is shown as an isolated product still on a black background: the audio output list presents all destinations with the same visual weight, the current device marked only by a selection icon and the speakers grouped under a section title, regardless of whether they are near or far (sheet 0014, q0120).
- Three hit target scales are compared in a direct sequence of frames: the dense grid of small icons on the home screen, the large circular trackpad of the remote with few buttons, and the calculator with a large display and dark gray and orange buttons (sheet 0015, q0133 to q0135).
- The share sheet is organized in stacked horizontal bands, with an item header, a row of avatars of suggested people where some carry a green indicator, a row of destination icons and text actions at the end; between two frames the first person on the list changes, which the notes read as a sign that the list updates on its own (sheet 0005, q0038 to q0040 and q0043).
- A design principle appears written over a composition of physical objects on the table, in two short lines of black text, without a formal slide frame (sheet 0012, q0104 to q0105).
- The identity of each of the five presenters comes through the setting: each change of person comes with a full cut of desk, decor, background computer and clothing, never with a gradual transition, and the opening presenter returns to the initial setting to close, followed by the closing slide with a concluding sentence and two cross references to other sessions with the year alongside (synthesis of sheets 0001 to 0017; sheet 0017, q0145 to q0151).

Visual proportion: according to the notes, most of the time is the presenter in a fixed studio shot, but nearly every sheet intercuts visual material, with the isolated product still on a black background standing out as a recurring device (sheets 0005, 0006, 0009, 0010, 0011, 0012, 0014, 0015 and 0016); sheets 0002 and 0003 are almost entirely images of devices and screens without a presenter, and software screens in real use, held in hand, appear in sheets 0004, 0005, 0006, 0007, 0009 to 0014 and 0016.

Recorded divergences or limits: several frames are noted as illegible or only partially legible, and therefore not cited, among them the small window on the Macintosh screen (sheet 0002, q0015), a transition text in the corner of the screen (sheet 0009, q0073), two colored screens held in hand (sheet 0010, q0089; sheet 0014, q0123) and a pattern of dots in an arc (sheet 0011, q0098). The identification of the old opening computer is recorded with uncertainty, as being one model or another (sheet 0001, q0009). The green light effect on sheet 0007 is explicitly marked as an illustration and not as a screen capture. On sheet 0016 the record notes that the name of the item being searched for changes between frames, from one item to another, along with the direction of the arrow.
