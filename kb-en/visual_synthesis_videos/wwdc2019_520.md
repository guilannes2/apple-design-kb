# wwdc2019_520: Introducing Core Haptics

### What the images show

Basis: 18 of 18 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.

- The whole talk runs on the same slide skeleton: small title in the upper left corner, a vertical list in light gray over a dark background and one item at a time in white and bold, with the presenter small, centered or to the right, below or beside the text. The rotation of the active item is visible frame by frame in the dynamic parameters list (sheet 0014, q0118 to q0120) and in the AHAP list (sheet 0015, q0128 to q0132). The speech states the topics; only the image shows the pace of revelation.
- The new API is placed within the system by a block diagram with color by layer: gray for the app, blue for the existing APIs (AVFoundation, CoreMIDI, UIKit and UIFeedbackGenerator), green for the band that gathers AudioToolbox, SystemSound, AudioUnits and Core Haptics, and red for Audio Hardware and Haptics Hardware, with a camera cut closing in on the green and red blocks (sheet 0001, q0008 to q0009; sheet 0002, q0010 to q0011). Green stays reserved for the subject of the talk throughout it.
- Hardware support is shown as evidence, not as a claim: a row of six iPhones with a real springboard screen under the text "Same feel across products" (sheet 0002, q0013), followed by a photo of the inside of an opened iPhone with a rounded green outline box pointing to the component and labeled "TAPTIC ENGINE" (q0015 and q0017). It is a measurement annotation applied to a physical part, not to a screen layout.
- The choice between the two APIs becomes a two-column table revealed at different times: first only the "UIFeedbackGenerator" column with its three attributes, then the "Core Haptics" column appears alongside it, with the title on the right in pure white and the one on the left in gray, shifting the focus (sheet 0003, q0021 to q0023).
- The duality between audio and haptics is built device by device in the same row: first an iPhone with a green circle on the bottom button labeled as the haptic home button, then the Apple Watch enters with a green circle on the crown and a third device with the UIDatePicker screen, and a frame closes on that calendar screen with the "None" and "Busy" options (sheet 0004, q0028 to q0033).
- The event types appear as columns revealed from left to right, each with a blue icon carrying the metaphor: a judge's gavel for HapticTransient, a violin for HapticContinuous and AudioContinuous, a speaker for AudioCustom (sheet 0007, q0056 to q0060). The event itself had already been drawn earlier as a green box with the fields Time, Type and EventParameters positioned over a horizontal timeline marked from "0.0" to "Time" (sheet 0006, q0054; sheet 0007, q0055, with three boxes at different heights and positions).
- The perceptual sharpness ruler fills in stages: the scale from 0.0 to 1.0 starts empty, gains the extreme labels "Round, organic" and "Crisp, precise", then receives a blue marker at 1.0 with the flashlight button and, next, a second marker further left with a framed thumbnail of the App Switcher, the two examples on the same ruler (sheet 0007, q0061 to q0063; sheet 0008, q0064 to q0067).
- The recommended playback flow is a synchronized diagram: on the left the numbered list growing from 1 to 7, on the right the class boxes with a fixed color by type (pattern in blue, player with a neutral outline, engine in orange), connected by red arrows, each gaining a state label in parentheses and a green arrow pointing from the active step to the corresponding object. The engine moves to "(Running)" at step 4, the player to "(Running)" at step 5, the balloon changes from "Done?" to "Player is done" and the two boxes end at "(Stopped)" (sheet 0010, q0082 to q0089).
- The code is presented with an attention cursor: a block in a dark blue background editor, a comment in gray always above the relevant line, and a light blue background band that shifts line by line as the speech advances, from "engine = try CHHapticEngine()" to the stoppedHandler, then to "try engine.start()" (sheet 0012, q0100 to q0105). At one point the highlight covers three lines at once, the whole parameter block of the audio event (sheet 0013, q0109 to q0110), different from the single-line pattern of the earlier sheets.
- There is a sequence of a real, non-rendered demo: a hand holding a physical iPhone lying against a light gray background, with a red circle on the screen that runs from the bottom left corner to the top right corner as the device is tilted, and the word "Demo" overlaid on the first frame (sheet 0011, q0092 to q0094). It is the only stretch of the video with a physical device in use, distinct from the digital-frame captures used in the rest.
- The effect of a dynamic parameter is proven by two stacked charts with the same time axis: above, the "Intensity Parameter" line gains a step starting at 0.5, below, the "Event Intensity" chart shows thin blue bars for transients and an orange block for the continuous one, and that block loses height and opacity exactly in the affected region (sheet 0014, q0123 to q0126).
- The AHAP file is typed live on screen, growing in parts: first only the version key highlighted, then the "Pattern" array with a HapticTransient event at 0.0 (sheet 0015, q0133 to q0134), then the empty parameters array receives HapticIntensity 0.8 and HapticSharpness 0.4, then a second HapticContinuous event enters at 0.5 and finally the duration key of 0.25 (sheet 0016, q0136 to q0139). Right after, the same values reappear as Intensity and Sharpness charts, with the blue bar at 0.0 and the orange block between 0.5 and 0.75, visually replicating what had just been typed (q0140).
- The structure of the format is summarized in a tree diagram with nodes in a light blue card and a light shadow, with a root at Version and Pattern and three parallel branches, Event, dynamic Parameter and dynamic ParameterCurve, each descending into its fields (sheet 0016, q0141 to q0143). It is the only hierarchical diagram in the video.
- The change of speaker is marked visually by a credit slide with name and role in small text in the corner, and the transition is captured in an intermediate frame with an enlarged text fragment behind the new presenter (sheet 0009, q0075 to q0077; the same device reappears at the closing, sheet 0017, q0147).
- The closing uses two questions in large centered typography over a black screen, with no other graphic element, as a rhetorical pause between sections (sheet 0017, q0149 to q0150), followed by the "Summary" slide accumulating six items, the link slide with the labs schedule table, and the final card with the white apple and the copyright text (sheet 0018, q0154 to q0161).

Visual proportion: across the 18 sheets, slides and code captures predominate, with the presenter alone appearing in isolated transition frames; there is a single real demonstration segment with a device in hand (sheet 0011, q0092 to q0094) and augmented reality app captures filling the frame in sheets 0005 and 0006, plus app thumbnails in an iPhone frame in sheets 0008, 0009 and 0017.

Recorded divergences or limits: in sheet 0001 the agenda slide is described as having five items, but only four are listed in the notes (Expressing content, Our first haptics, Dynamic parameters, Apple Haptic Audio Pattern), and the change of bolded item between q0004 and q0006 is recorded as repeating "Dynamic parameters". In sheet 0017 the names of the HapticSampler buttons are illegible in the thumbnail and were noted only as an approximation.
