### What the images show
Basis: 7 of 7 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.
- Line-pointer annotation labels point to specific regions of the same notification capture, naming each part instead of leaving the description only in words: "Short Look" over the lock screen banner, repositioned between frames, then "Long Look" over the expanded card and "Quick Actions" pointing only to the footer buttons (sheet 0002, q0015, q0016 and q0018; sheet 0003, q0019 to q0020).
- Anatomy of the compact banner: app icon, category name, relative time and two summary lines over the lock screen with time and date (sheet 0002, q0014). The expanded version of the same event adds a mini day agenda with another scheduled appointment, full address, a close button in the corner and three actions stacked in full-width buttons (q0017).
- A two-column glossary slide fixes the three concepts and the question each layer answers, term in bold on the left and guiding question on the right (sheet 0003, q0023).
- The same card with two states repeats across almost all sheets, and what changes from app to app are the footer actions: accept, maybe and decline in Calendar (sheet 0002, q0017), like and comment in Photos (sheet 0004, q0028 to q0030), call back and delete in Phone (q0036), four sharing durations in Find Friends (sheet 0005, q0039 and q0040), play now, queue and archive in the podcast (sheet 0006, q0050 and q0051), play audio and snooze alert in the door camera (q0053 and q0054).
- A destructive action is marked by color in the footer of the email card, with the trash button in red next to the neutral mark-as-read button (sheet 0003, q0026).
- Embedded media changes according to the content type: compact audio player with progress bar plus the full transcript in text in the voicemail (sheet 0004, q0036), map with a location pin over a building in Find Friends (sheet 0005, q0039), large photo and bulleted article text with emojis in the news app (sheet 0006, q0048), video thumbnail from the camera showing the sidewalk and a person in the distance (q0053).
- The most complex example is an entire conversation rendered inside the card, with a history of bubbles, emoji, a visible QWERTY keyboard and a row of one-word quick replies, with the content updating frame by frame, text appearing in the typing field and new lines entering the conversation without leaving the notification (sheet 0005, q0042 to q0044).
- Each app keeps its own typography and accent color within the same card format, with the app title, media, text and footer actions (sheet 0006), including the podcast with title, duration, description and cover thumbnail in the corner (q0050 and q0051).
- A red counter badge at stage size opens and closes the talk as a graphic metaphor, jumping from one to one thousand and forty-five at the opening and reappearing with almost two thousand at the close, without being an app capture (sheet 0001, q0005 to q0007; sheet 0007, q0058).
- The recap slide uses color hierarchy, with the line in focus in strong white and the others grayed out, and the highlight migrating from the first to the second line between frames (sheet 0007, q0055 to q0056).
- Transitions between examples are made through double exposure, with the next notification appearing translucent over the presenter's silhouette before taking over the screen (sheet 0003, q0025 and q0027).
Visual proportion: most of the frames are captures of real notifications on the lock screen or expanded card, with few text slides and short blocks of the presenter alone between the examples.
Recorded divergences or limits: the notes point out that the notification from the tips app teaching how to press to expand a notification appears on screen without being mentioned in the speech of the segment (sheet 0004, q0032 to q0035), and that the number shown in the closing badge is not mentioned in the speech (sheet 0007, q0058).
