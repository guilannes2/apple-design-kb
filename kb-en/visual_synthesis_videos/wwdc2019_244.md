### What the images show
Basis: 8 of 8 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.

- The typographic scale diagram is the central visual piece: two columns linked by a horizontal arrow, the left one with the 11 style names all in the same small, uniform body size, the right one with the same names each rendered in its real size and weight, from Large Title to Footnote (sheet 0002, q0013). The speech only lists the styles; the proportion between them exists only on screen.
- The code slides use progressive line-by-line highlighting to guide the reading: the "Custom Fonts" block first appears whole and without highlighting, then with a blue band over the conditional that looks up the font in the dictionary, then with the band shifted to the fallback line inside the else (sheet 0002, q0016 to q0018). The Swift is set on a dark blue background with colored syntax, keywords in pink and values in green.
- Design tool shown in use, and not just mentioned: a MacBook with Xcode open displays the "Environment Overrides" panel with a slider labeled Dynamic Type and an accessibility switch, next to the iPhone simulator running the sample app (sheet 0003, q0019 to q0022). Between q0019 and q0020 the overlaid code block leaves the scene and reveals the panel in full. The speech mentions the panel in passing; the on-screen arrangement is what shows how it is used.
- Anatomy of the list row used as a test case: in the fictional peanut app, each row has a heart icon on the left, the product name, a row of rating stars just below the name and a thumbnail of the package on the right (sheet 0003, q0019). On sheet 0006 the same list appears with a colored circle in place of the heart, to the left of the product name (q0049).
- Before-and-after pair of Differentiate Without Color recorded in the same layout: the list shows green, yellow and red circles next to each product (sheet 0006, q0049) and, at the end of the sheet, the same circles become a green check mark and a red or orange "X", with one item gaining a green outline, the same list layout kept (q0054).
- The color blindness simulation is also shown over the interface itself: q0047 shows the app's list in grayed-out tones, simulating color-blind vision, and q0048 shows the same list in normal color next to the presenter (sheet 0006).
- Four twelve-slice color wheels lined up horizontally, labeled below as normal vision, deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia, each of the last three with a small reference color circle underneath, concretely show distinct hues collapsing into similar tones (sheet 0005, q0044). Protanopia with an additional highlight in the legend already appears on the next sheet (sheet 0006, q0046).
- The Settings screens are used as proof that the feature is new, and the items enter one per frame: the Motion list first appears with only Reduce Motion and Auto-Play Message Effects, then gains Auto-Play Video Previews (sheet 0004, q0031 to q0032), then gains "Prefer Cross-fade Transitions" marked with a green outline (sheet 0005, q0041), and in Display and Text Size the Differentiate Without Color item appears with the same green outline (sheet 0006, q0051). The green outline is the fixed graphic device that points to the new item; in q0051 it appears together with the corresponding code block overlaid on the screen.
- Each feature is anchored to an app screen before the API: a track list from an album with a thumbnail cover, Play and Shuffle buttons side by side and a star icon plus a cloud icon to the left of each track, for Dynamic Type (sheet 0001, q0006 and q0007); a weather forecast in a vertical list with day, condition icon and high and low aligned in columns, for Reduce Motion (sheet 0003, q0023 and q0024); and the App Store with the featured section bringing a large video card and, below, a grid of two game icons with a GET button to the right of each name, for the autoplay (sheet 0004, q0029).
- The statistic is glued to the screen that exemplifies it, not on a separate slide: the text "1 in 3" and the line about sensitivity to motion appear next to the same weather app screen already on screen in the previous frame (sheet 0003, q0023 to q0024).
- The principle slides are pure text, without ornament, almost always with the presenter small on screen: "If text can be dynamic, it should be dynamic." (sheet 0002, q0010), "Don't truncate text" (q0011), "Scale content glyphs" accompanied by an iPhone with a list of health categories (q0012) and "When possible, do not rely on color alone." (sheet 0007, q0057). "Mirror by Default", with a bold title and two sentences in an unbulleted list, is the only one of these frames recorded without the presenter visible (sheet 0005, q0037).
- The final case study is a game in an isometric setting on a tablet, with stylized rocks, a small robot and rhomboid and square shapes on the ground, each with a symbol floating above it (arrow, X, diamond), plus a panel of two circular buttons in the top right corner and a clock icon in the top left corner (sheet 0007, q0060 to q0062). These are the symbols that replace color, seen on the game's own screen.
- The title slide sets the session's graphic identity: dark background, white left-aligned text, "#WWDC19" small in the top corner, the presenter's name and title in a smaller size below the title (sheet 0001, q0002). The closing keeps the same sober register, with the Apple logo centered in white on black and a small block of legal text below (sheet 0008, q0067).

Visual proportion: across the eight sheets there is constant alternation between the presenter in close-up without a slide and a device screen, diagram, code block or text slide, and there is no recorded sheet that is only the presenter, meaning practically the entire video carries supporting visual material.

Recorded divergences or limits: the notes point out frames that are practically identical, with no perceptible difference between them (q0014 and q0015, q0020 and q0021, q0035 and q0036, q0044 and q0045). The first frame, q0001, is entirely dark blue, with no visible content. In q0034 the background text appears cut off by the sheet's edge, legible only in part. The "Summary" slide of sheet 0008 lists two points in writing, while the speech summarizes three.
