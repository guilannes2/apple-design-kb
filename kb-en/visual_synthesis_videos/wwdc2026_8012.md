### What the images show
Basis: 43 of 43 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.

- The tool always appears in the same three-panel layout, layer and group tree on the left, central canvas with the rendered icon and property inspector on the right, a stable pattern across every sheet with the app on screen (sheets 0002 to 0007, 0021, 0022 and 0040). The images show the exact names and fixed order of the inspector fields, which the talk treats in general terms.
- The icon's construction hierarchy is legible in the tree: layers named one by one inside nested groups, with indentation, and the selected state marked by a blue highlight on the tree row and a blue outline around the corresponding element on the canvas (sheets 0002, 0003 q0022 and q0023, 0004 and 0006).
- The inspector changes according to the selected level: with the group selected, the Liquid Glass section appears with Mode, Specular, Blur, Refraction, Translucency and Shadow; with an individual layer selected, Opacity, Blend Mode, Fill, a Liquid Glass effects toggle, the source image file and Layout with position and scale appear (sheet 0003, q0022 to q0027). In other words, the glass parameters appear at the group level, and at the layer level there's the fill color and only the toggle that decides whether it receives the effect.
- The dropdown menus reveal the closed option sets: Fill lists Automatic, Solid, Gradient, System Light and System Dark (sheet 0003, q0021); Specular lists Off, Automatic, Inside and Outline (sheet 0004, q0030); and Blend Mode lists ten modes in a vertical sequence, with a selection mark on the active one and a blue highlight on the item under the cursor (sheet 0021, q0181 and q0186 to q0188).
- Refraction is a two-dimensional control with two percentage values alongside it, and the frame sequence follows the cursor moving while the petals go from smooth edges to edges that are increasingly wavy and curved outward (sheet 0005, q0037 to q0042), with a pair of frames where the value changes without a visible change in the shape (q0041 to q0042). The notes record the exact numeric pairs for each frame.
- Other fine adjustments appear with the value box being edited: Translucency being typed while the icon stays stable on the canvas (sheet 0005, q0043 to q0045) and Shadow with the Chromatic sub-item being edited while progressive zoom makes the glow around the petal edges more visible (sheet 0006, q0046 to q0048).
- The adaptation of a single file to multiple contexts is demonstrated in sequence with the same icon: rounded square frame, circular frame, back to square, a colored version over a dark background and a monochrome version in white and gray over a light background (sheet 0006, q0049 to q0054).
- A floating "Appearance" panel over the canvas brings together a Light and Dark toggle, a Tinted switch and a horizontal rainbow gradient bar with a cursor to choose the tint color, a component the talk doesn't describe visually (sheet 0007, q0055 and q0056).
- Testing glass against a real background happens right in the tool: the canvas background switches from solid gray to a colored gradient image filling the whole area, with the icon overlaid showing the background's refraction through the material, and the toolbar gains extra preview controls (sheet 0007, q0057 to q0058, and back to gray at q0061).
- Composition by platform is shown in the maps app file: the icon appears as a rounded-corner square with a dashed circular guide overlaid indicating the crop area, then as a full circle with the center marker recentered, and then back to the square (sheet 0040, q0356 to q0359). The layers in this file are named by function and destination, including suffixes that indicate the watch variant.
- Canvas zoom is used as an inspection tool and not just for navigation, going well above the natural size to check the selection outline and shadow glow at the edges (sheets 0004, q0031, and 0006, q0046 to q0048).
- Audience questions come in as a standardized graphic card, a rounded-corner rectangle with a semi-transparent dark background, white text left-aligned in the bottom third, the author's name in bold and a vote count in green alongside it (sheets 0008, 0010, 0012, 0017, 0020, 0024, 0025, 0027, 0029, 0030, 0033 to 0035, 0039 and 0041). The card fades in and out gradually, visible frame by frame (sheet 0028, q0244 to q0247, and sheet 0030, q0264 to q0267).
- The transition between studio and demo is made through an intermediate frame where the presenters appear partially transparent behind the enlarged icon, in the four instances where the recording enters or leaves a screen in the first block of sheets (0002, 0007, 0021 and 0022). The entry into the sheet 0040 screen doesn't carry this device, and its exit is a simple fade (q0359).
- The set design is a conversation table, not a presentation: a curved light-wood table, a vertical-slat panel in the background with an illuminated sign, table microphones and open laptops in front of each participant, with the camera alternating between a wide shot of all five and shots of two or one. The notes describe this setting in detail in the sheets of the final stretch, and the same studio table with five panelists already appears from sheet 0001 onward.
- The closing overlays the Apple logo watermark and a copyright notice in small centered text over the panel shot (sheet 0043, q0380 to q0381).

Visual proportion: of 43 sheets, the app's interface appears in nine (0002 to 0007, 0021, 0022 and 0040); the other 34 sheets are panelists talking in studio, with overlaid question cards and an opening poster.

Recorded divergences or limits: the notes point out that in long stretches the speech deals with visual topics such as blend modes, contrast and layers with no demonstration on screen at all, with sheet 0040 being the sole exception in the final stretch (sheets 0023, 0026, 0031, 0032, 0036 to 0038 and 0042). The text of some questions is recorded in abbreviated form in the notes, with ellipses, because the card showed only part of the statement (sheets 0024 and 0027).
