### What the images show
Basis: 20 of 20 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.

- The sample app appears on iPad in a three-column layout, with an album navigation panel on the left, content area in the center and a vertical strip of sticker categories on the right in a grid of small icons, plus a top toolbar with monochrome icons aligned to the right (sheet 0001, q0006 to q0009). Between q0007 and q0008 the left panel disappears and the photo takes up more width.
- The topic slides use the same fixed list with hierarchy by weight and color, active item in strong black and the rest in light gray, and advance by changing only the highlighted item (sheets 0002, 0006, 0007, 0011, 0015). From q0014 to q0015 the single title slide turns into the list of four topics, and from q0061 to q0062 a subitem becomes bold while the next one appears below it (sheet 0007).
- A green circular "NEW" badge stays fixed in the top right corner of the code blocks in dozens of frames, from sheets 0008 through 0019, marking which APIs are new. It's a recurring graphic element that the narration doesn't verbalize at every occurrence.
- The code is revealed line by line, from the empty structure to the complete implementation. On sheet 0009 the document class starts from a shell (q0073), gains the conformance and the static list of supported types (q0077, q0078), then the snapshot method (q0079, q0080) and finally the writer, with a floating card detailing the snapshot struct overlaid on the code (q0081). On sheet 0010 the write method's signature is revealed in stages, first the declaration and then the internal comments (q0083 to q0087).
- Categorical color separates the protocol pairs: cards side by side in green for the write side and blue for the read side, each with a centered title and signature lines on a darker background inside the card (sheet 0010, q0090). The same color code returns as a diagram of stacked cards connected by a straight arrow (sheet 0011, q0091).
- Before and after pair of toolbar: at q0040 the iPhone bar is compact and at q0041 the overflow menu opens anchored to the ellipsis button, with list items aligned to the left over a glass-like translucent background. From q0042 to q0043 the undo and redo buttons leave the overflow and start appearing directly in the bar, highlighted in blue (sheet 0005).
- The highlighted tab appears as an isolated circular cart button in the bottom right corner, outside the tab bar, while the other four tabs stay grouped on the left (sheet 0004, q0033, q0034). Around it, the store uses a four-column grid of colored icons and a product card with a purple badge, title, description and action button.
- The argument about type-checking time is supported almost entirely by diagrams. A tree of blue boxes for concrete types and white boxes with a border for containers grows layer by layer (sheet 0018, q0159 to q0161), reaches rows of repeated pyramids occupying the full width of the frame (sheet 0019, q0163 to q0165) and suddenly collapses into a single vertical chain, with the alternative branches first fading to gray and then disappearing for good (q0166, q0167).
- Compiler errors appear as a light red band with an icon on the left, anchored to the exact line of code, both in the use-before-initialization case (sheet 0017, q0153) and in the type-checking time case (sheet 0018, q0157, q0158). The image pins down where the error occurs more precisely than the speech does.
- Circular version badges mark behavior change without code change: the same snippet appears identical with a gray "26.0" badge (q0145, q0146) and then with a green "27.0" badge (q0148, q0149) on sheet 0017.
- A blue rectangular annotation with an arrow points to a specific line and names it as the observable class instance (sheet 0016, q0142 to q0144), a didactic convention used to link text and code.
- The code appears overlaid with partial transparency over the device screen running the app, leaving the result visible in the background (sheets 0003, 0012, 0013 and 0016). In other passages the notes describe code and screen side by side (sheets 0005, 0006, 0014) or isolated code, with no app screen (sheets 0009, 0010). At q0106 and q0107 a small gray credit in the footer cites the collections library used.
- The reordering is demonstrated by the difference between frames: from q0102 to q0103 the order of the side panel items changes, and from q0107 to q0108 the same panel goes from list mode, with a square thumbnail and name, to grid mode with smaller icons in several columns (sheet 0012).
- The device frame changes according to the use case, iPad for the store and the library, iPhone for toolbar and swipe actions, MacBook for reordering between list and grid, reinforcing platform adaptation through the image.
- The closing slide uses a photograph of colorful physical objects on a wooden table as the background, with the text revealed line by line in a column on the left (sheet 0020, q0176, q0177).

Visual proportion: most of the recorded frames are Swift code, device screen and slide, with presenter frames interspersed throughout almost every sheet and the two together in the opening, at theme transitions and at the close (sheets 0001, 0006, 0015, 0020).

Recorded divergences or limits: the notes mark some frames as uncertain, a small circular indicator with a time over the scene described as a possible notification or tooltip (sheet 0013, q0111), a solid purple rectangle with no interface element treated as a probable transition or loading frame (sheet 0015, q0132) and an almost blank frame with barely legible ghost text (sheet 0014, q0126). On sheet 0006 the notes record that the code evolves from q0046 to q0048 while the iPhone screen visually stays the same across the three frames.
