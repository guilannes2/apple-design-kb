### What the images show
Basis: 8 of 8 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.
- The opening builds the problem through visual accumulation, with no text: three iPhone-proportion rectangles, then three iPad-proportion ones, then the two groups overlapped in different tones, then five rectangles lying down with a blue stripe indicating landscape, and finally a grid of fifteen rectangles in three rows by increasing size, at which point the label "Size Classes" appears (sheet 0001, q0004 to q0009). The devices are drawn as simple outlines, with no screen element at all.
- The two categories are named next to the corresponding drawings, with measurement arrows under each rectangle: "Compact" with three narrow vertical rectangles on the left, "Regular" with three larger ones in grayish blue on the right (sheet 0002, q0013).
- The central explanatory device is a two-axis matrix, compact and regular width at the top, compact and regular height on the side, filled with blue blocks of different opacities, and this matrix returns as a backdrop at other moments, including behind a real iPhone showing email in split (sheets 0002, 0003 and 0007).
- The readability margin gets a direct marking over a real screen: a thin pink vertical stripe runs along the left margin of the open email, marking where the text begins, and stays stable for four frames in a row (sheet 0003, q0021 to q0024).
- The type scale appears as a named list stacked in decreasing size, with the annotation "Default Size" pointing to the middle of it and the names going from Large and Title 1 down to Caption (sheet 0004, q0034). Before that, the same style table appears overlaid on the app's text and then in a wide grid with the columns repeated at increasing sizes (q0032 and q0033).
- Localization is shown rather than mentioned: the same email app appears in a different script, with long text, and next to it the column of typographic styles to compare the behavior (sheet 0004, q0031 and q0032).
- The custom font is compared against visible numeric data: the word "Body" appears twice side by side, one in the system font and the other serifed, captioned with the size in points and the name of each font, under the slide title about the iOS 11 Dynamic Type API (sheet 0005, q0038 and q0039).
- The table component is shown in wireframe with its states open simultaneously: four columns repeating the same row pattern with title and detail, selection indicators, a red "Delete" button revealed by the swipe and a button label field, which exposes the component's anatomy in normal, editing and swipe mode (sheet 0006, q0047, picked up again on sheet 0007).
- Master and Detail are labeled on top of the mockup and then one of them is erased to isolate the focus: iPhone and iPad side by side with the same contacts app, first the two labels together over the iPad's columns, then the "Master View" label disappears, the list column turns faded gray and the iPhone leaves the frame, leaving only the detail highlighted (sheet 0006, q0051 to q0054).
- Component adaptation by screen size is shown through the difference between two frames: the iPad goes from a single column to two columns, each with its own "Parent Title / Title" navigation bar, and the bottom bar swaps the geometric icons for the text labels "Action 1", "Action 2" and "Action 3", while the iPhone keeps the icons (sheet 0007, q0055 and q0056).
- The work tool enters the screen: a Sketch capture with a layers panel, ready-made screen size templates and an interface component library (sheet 0006, q0048). A few frames earlier, the "Apple UI Design Resources" page appears open in a browser with the list of resources (sheet 0005, q0044).
- The topics slide works as a structure marker with hierarchy shown only through color, the item in focus in full white and the others in gray, and in the recap each topic comes back accompanied by the thumbnail of its own diagram, the matrix for size classes, the style list for Dynamic Type and the table wireframe for UIKit Elements (sheet 0007, q0058 to q0063).
- The closing is a single frame with simplified gray pictograms of iPhone and iPad separated by a red heart, with no screen detail at all (sheet 0008, q0064).
Visual proportion: almost all the frames feature a diagram, device mockup or slide on screen, with the presenter alone only in short stretches of sheets 0002, 0003, 0004, 0005 and 0006.
