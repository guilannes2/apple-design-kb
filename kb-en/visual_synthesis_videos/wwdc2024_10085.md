### What the images show
Basis: 19 of 19 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.

- The same game scene is displayed inside real device frames to visually support the idea of a single platform (sheet 0001, q0004 to q0008): a robotic character in a green landscape from three angles inside the MacBook frame, then the same landscape on iPad and iPhone with a smaller iPad alongside. In q0009 the screen moves to the developer website page with the title "Designing for games", a clear typographic hierarchy and a green highlight block with a controller icon.
- A topic card functions as a progress index throughout the talk (sheets 0002, 0004, 0006 to 0009, 0012, 0013 and 0017 to 0019): a fixed title at the top, a vertical list where the current item is in bold black and the rest in light gray, with the items revealing themselves gradually. At the end of the first block all five items appear in solid black (sheet 0012, q0107 and q0108), marking the close of the section.
- The installation error is shown as a failed interface (sheet 0003, q0019 and q0020): a centered modal alert over a darkened background, an uppercase title, body text stating 10.5 GB and an orange download button, with a red X alongside marking the pattern as an example to avoid; the same alert repeats in the following frame with a darker background screen.
- The installation diagram receives annotations between frames (sheet 0003, q0021 and q0022): a horizontal bar divided into blue labeled "Playable" and gray labeled "Undownloaded", and in the following frame the annotations "15 min" and "Background download" appear below, which did not exist before. Then the same bar appears overlaid on the footer of the real gameplay and the blue section visibly advances with each frame (q0024 to q0026).
- The chapters screen encodes state through opacity and button color (sheet 0003, q0027; sheet 0004, q0028 and q0029): cards in a column with the locked chapter at reduced opacity and a wait-for-download notice, an orange "REPLAY" button for completed and a green "CONTINUE" for the current one, and callout lines connecting the bar to the label "Show progress" and the buttons to the label "Allow replay".
- Callout lines pointing from screen elements to labels outside it are a recurring device (sheets 0004, 0007 and 0008), used for "Show progress", "Allow replay", "Safe area", "Home indicator", "Dynamic Island" and "Rounded corners". The notes record that this technical annotation pattern is not described in the speech.
- Onboarding fields disappear between two frames to show what the system already knows (sheet 0005, q0038 to q0039): the grid of pill selectors loses "Screen Size" and "Aspect Ratio" and now starts at "HDR Mode". Next, the side-by-side iPhone and MacBook comparison shows the version with Lighting, Reflections and Effects all set to "HIGH" marked with a red X and the version at "MEDIUM" without a mark (q0043 to q0045).
- The problem of scaling the whole interface up appears as a wrong-and-right pair (sheet 0006, q0051 to q0054): the same scene on iPad Pro and iPad mini with the device name below, HUD with virtual joystick, action button and health bar becoming disproportionate or cut off at the edges of the iPad mini with a red X, and the final version with the same controls repositioned and resized with no error mark.
- The safe area is drawn over screens in full-screen mode (sheet 0007, q0060 to q0063): six device frames with the same colored image with no interface, and over them semitransparent rectangles labeled "Safe area" with lines pointing to the Home indicator at the bottom and the Dynamic Island at the top; in q0063 two rectangles of different sizes overlap on the same iPhone screen, suggesting variation between models.
- Camera framing decisions go through the same binary judgment (sheet 0008, q0064 to q0070): overlapping thumbnails compared with a green check and a red X depending on whether important content is cropped by the device's aspect ratio or not, including comparisons between MacBook and iPhone and between two vertical iPhone framings.
- The letterbox appears with different fills in the following frames (sheet 0009, q0075 to q0078): vertical bars that show the original scene with a checkered floor and fire, then turn white, then receive a dark blue gradient and return to the original scene, illustrating the options to fill with art or neutralize.
- Numeric measurements are always paired with the element at real size (sheets 0010 and 0011): a large number with the label "Default" or "Minimum" on the left and a device frame on the right showing the corresponding text or button. The frames viewed show "17pt Default" and "11pt Minimum" for iPhone and iPad with a sample mission paragraph (q0087 to q0090), and "44pt Default" with "28pt Minimum" for touch, appearing in two steps over the same menu (q0094 to q0095), then "28pt Default" for Mac with a mouse cursor resting on the "Chapters" button (q0097).
- The game menu differentiates primary from secondary action by color and size (sheet 0011, q0094 and q0095): orange rectangular buttons with rounded corners and centered white text for Chapters, Graphics, Audio and Controls, and below a section of items in lighter gray for Advanced, Account and Credits.
- Scroll view is demonstrated with real scrolling between frames (sheet 0012, q0102 to q0103): on the "Guides" screen on a landscape iPhone, the column of buttons on the left completely swaps items and the panel on the right also changes content, with the same fixed image and text area kept, and a horizontal scroll bar at the bottom.
- Two flow diagrams contrast physical controller and touch (sheet 0014, q0122 to q0125): gray rectangular boxes for "Input" and purple for "Game" connected by a single horizontal arrow in the case of the controller with TV, and the same pair with a double arrow in the case of touch, there with the virtual HUD (D-pad, action buttons and two translucent joystick circles) overlaid on the game scene on the iPhone.
- Controls are marked as failed, corrected and replaced by action glyphs in sequence (sheet 0017, q0146 to q0153): the HUD with "L2" and "R2" in the corners receives a red X, the same labels swap sides and the mark turns into a green check, then a two-column mapping diagram connects the physical button to an action icon (fist, X) and finally the final HUD appears with L2, R2, A and B. On sheet 0015 the same vocabulary of check and X is applied to the virtual joystick and to the drag camera; on sheets 0017 and 0018 the HUD gains and loses icons depending on the game context.

Visual proportion: across the 19 sheets the presenter appears alone in short, identifiable stretches (for example, sheet 0004, q0034 to q0036; sheet 0013, q0109; isolated frames on sheets 0011, 0012 and 0017); most of the frames are device mockups, game HUD, annotated diagrams, documentation pages or topic cards.

Recorded divergences or limits: on sheets 0010 and 0011 the notes record that the speech gives values that the frames viewed do not display. In q0088 the title switches to "Mac type sizes" but the Mac numbers still do not appear, and the synthesis notes the pair of 13pt and 10pt as implied and not seen; in the touch target block, the Mac's 20pt minimum cited in the speech also does not appear on screen.
