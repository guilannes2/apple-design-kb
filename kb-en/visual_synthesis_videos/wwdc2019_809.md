# wwdc2019_809: Designing iPad Apps for Mac

### What the images show

Basis: 25 of 25 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.

- The talk uses the same two-column agenda slide as a progress marker: the ten topics appear together and go from dim gray to white as they are covered, first only "Architecture" lit up (sheet 0006, q0047 to q0048), then the whole left column (sheet 0013, q0112 to q0113), then with "Gestures" and "Touch Bars" (sheet 0016, q0144) and finally everything in white (sheet 0020, q0177 to q0178; sheet 0024, q0212 to q0213). The technique is purely visual, the speech does not describe the slide's state.
- The difference between touch and cursor is demonstrated with two buttons on the same white screen: a blue button surrounded by a translucent circle larger than the control itself, representing the touch area, and below it a small rectangular button with the mouse cursor next to it, with the cursor changing position between frames (sheet 0004, q0031 to q0032). On the same sheet a gesture diagram appears with blue arrows labeled "Pan", "Rotate" and "Pinch" (q0033).
- The typography table is the frame with the most exposed measurements: on the left the "macOS" column with Medium 13pt, Small 11pt and Mini 9pt, on the right the column that starts as "iOS", with the styles named with no value, and in the next frame it becomes "iOS @77% Scale" with the readable points (LargeTitle 26.2pt, Title1 21.6pt, Body 13.0pt, Caption2 7.7pt) (sheet 0012, q0101 to q0103). Then the same scale is demonstrated inside a design tool: a text block with selection handles and the "W: 77.00%" and "H: 77.00%" indicators at the top (q0107 to q0108).
- The emphasis within this table moves from one frame to the next, going from "Headline" to "Mini" and the small styles, marking in bold exactly the sizes that the speech treats as risky on the Mac (sheet 0013, q0110 to q0111).
- The architecture translation is shown as a chain, not in words: bottom tab bar on the iPad with "Section 1", then a grid of folders and files under "Root Level", then the same grid inside a Mac window with a segmented control at the top, and finally the same window with a text sidebar (Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, Dessert) with no icon grid (sheet 0006, q0050 to q0054). The master-detail pattern reappears mirrored, a list with a chevron plus a detail panel on the iPad and a sidebar plus content area on the Mac (sheet 0007, q0062 to q0063; sheet 0008, q0064 to q0065).
- The window's focus state is proven by side-by-side comparison: two instances of the same photos window, one labeled inactive with the sidebar losing sharpness and marked with a pink circle with an X, the other labeled active with a sharp sidebar and a green circle, and the labels swapping sides between frames (sheet 0008, q0070 to q0072).
- Judgment of selection color appears as right-and-wrong pairs: four small sidebar mockups over the wallpaper, each pair with an item highlighted in a different color and a red X or a green check right below, and the color of the highlighted item changing between frames (sheet 0009, q0074 to q0075).
- The argument against actions on the bottom edge is staged with the system itself: an iPad screen with action icons isolated in the bottom corners, with no central bar (sheet 0009, q0081), and the same screen with a translucent panel at the bottom (sheet 0010, q0082), then a Mac desktop with the Dock occupying the bottom and an empty window floating just above it, in wide shot and zoomed in (sheet 0010, q0083 to q0084), and then the alternative, a translucent strip of icons anchored in the top-right corner (q0086 to q0087).
- Layout is studied in a low-fidelity blue wireframe, with rectangles marked by an X for content and thin lines for text: first the same iPad wireframe enlarged inside a MacBook, with a single empty content area, then the same MacBook with a side column of text lines and two content areas (sheet 0011, q0094 to q0096), and then the pair labeled "Primary View / Secondary View", "Objects / Object 1 Details" and "Master View / Detail View" (q0097 to q0099). The same sheet carries a dimension diagram of the 12.9-inch iPad Pro with "2048 px" and "2736 px" marked and a diagonal connecting the corners.
- The neutral color thesis is shown by removing color from the screen itself: the photos window loses the side color-reference panel and is left with a dark gray sidebar, with the thumbnails being the only colored element (sheet 0014, q0118 to q0121); right after, the System Preferences General panel appears with light and dark appearance, Accent color and Highlight color swatches (q0122). On the next sheet, a segmented control and a stepper appear orange under the "iOS 12" label and neutral under "iOS 13", and in the next frame a separate slide enters with named system color lists (FillColor, SecondaryFillColor, SeparatorColor and others), each name with its swatch (sheet 0015, q0128 to q0130).
- The gesture remapping becomes a paired diagram: "Tap" with a blue circle next to "Mouse Down" with an arrow cursor, and in the next frame "Pan" next to "Mouse Drag", with the blue circle shifted (sheet 0015, q0131 to q0133), followed by two blue circles over a photo grid suggesting a pinch on the trackpad (q0134 to q0135). The gesture that does not translate gets an explicit alert: a spinner with "Pull to Refresh" and a yellow exclamation circle (sheet 0016, q0139 to q0141). The mouse's gain appears in two stacked "AAPL" charts, one pointed to by a touch circle and the other by a hover cursor at the same data point (q0142 to q0143).
- The app icon is treated as a problem of shape and resolution: an empty white squircle template is inserted in the middle of a row of real app icons and then isolated over a checkered background, changing size between frames (sheet 0017, q0148 to q0153); generic white silhouettes and an extensive grid of named system icons serve as a reference for outline variety; and the same colored icon appears side by side labeled "16 x 16 pixels", simplified, against "Full Resolution", detailed (sheet 0018, q0155 to q0158), with the HIG page "Perspective and Textures" showing the camera angle and light diagram (q0159 to q0160).
- Contextual menus are shown working in real apps, with the commands readable one by one: the Photos menu on the Mac with "Get Info", "Rotate Clockwise", "Duplicate 1 Photo", "Hide 1 Photo", "Delete 1 Photo", and the equivalent on the iPad with "Copy", "Share", "Favorite", "Remove" (sheet 0019, q0164 to q0166). The recommendations appear in a slide that reveals one item at a time, dimming the ones already covered (q0170 to q0171), and the grouping by separator is illustrated by a mockup with "Command 1" through "Command 9" split into three blocks by dividing lines (sheet 0020, q0173 to q0175).
- The menu bar is broken down on screen: a two-column table "Object" and "Action" as a cataloging exercise, then the standard macOS bar with each menu opened in sequence, from the app menu to "Format" and then "Help" (sheet 0021, q0183 to q0189). The custom menu argument is visual: a mailbox and an envelope side by side, each with its own list of commands with no overlap, and the real Mail bar showing "Mailbox" and "Message" in addition to the standard menus (sheet 0022, q0193 to q0197). Menu stability is illustrated with two instances of the Finder's "Edit" menu side by side, each with a "Demo Script" document icon below, first with no selection highlighted and then with the left icon selected in blue (sheet 0023, q0203 to q0204), and Keynote's "Arrange" menu illustrates organization by workflow (q0199 to q0200).
- Real documentation pages appear in the browser inside the presentation, not just cited: "Touch Bar Overview" (sheet 0017, q0146), "Perspective and Textures" (sheet 0018, q0160), "Reserved and Expected Keyboard Shortcuts" with a shortcut table (sheet 0024, q0209) and "iPad Apps on Mac" with the "Before You Start" section (sheet 0025, q0217 to q0218), plus a design tool window with a component library in collapsible sections (q0219).

Visual proportion: in the 25 sheets the presenter appears alone in isolated frames distributed throughout the talk, generally at topic changes; the body of the frames is slide, mockup, wireframe or a real app window, with the scale demonstration at 77% in a design tool in sheets 0012 and 0013, an interface component library window in sheet 0025 and no live app demo recorded.

Recorded divergences or limits: in sheet 0024 the final agenda slide appears with the title "Bringing Your iPad App to Mac", different from the title "Designing iPad Apps for Mac" used in the earlier agenda slides. In sheet 0022 (q0192) a menu called "Custom" appears open in the menu bar with no visible command content.
