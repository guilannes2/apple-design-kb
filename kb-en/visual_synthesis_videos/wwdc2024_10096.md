### What the images show
Basis: 17 of 17 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.

- The presentation carries a fixed section menu that the narration never mentions: a left-aligned text column with "Setting", "Interactions" and "Audience", only the current item in black and bold and the rest in light gray, reappearing at every block change (sheets 0002, 0006, 0007, 0008, 0011, 0013 and 0015). The subitems enter the same column with the same treatment, for example "Place content magically" becoming active while the neighbors turn gray (sheet 0004, q0029 to q0036).
- The three visionOS content types are explained through a single graphic vocabulary, not through screen capture: white background, thin-line human silhouette, elliptical shadow on the ground marking anchoring, and solid or translucent blue shapes for the virtual content, with the label always in the upper left corner. The sequence shows "Window" as a solid blue rectangle, "Immersive" as a wireframe cube with spheres around it and "Custom RealityKit portal" as a translucent rectangle with the dinosaur's silhouette inside, keeping the same spatial composition in all three (sheet 0003, q0021, q0022 and q0026).
- The adaptation of the same portal to different spaces appears as a side-by-side comparison in isometric diagrams: a large, well-lit room, then a second identical room next to it forming a pair, then "Limited space" as a dark, cramped room where the only light comes from the blue rectangle, and finally the three variations reduced to thumbnails in a repeated row (sheet 0005, q0039 to q0045).
- The portal concept is never shown loose in the environment. In every scene about the real room, the dinosaur content appears inside a horizontal rectangle with straight edges fixed to the wall, which visually supports the portal idea that the narration describes (sheets 0002 q0017, 0003, 0004 and 0012).
- The experience's actual onboarding is shown frame by frame over the door of a household cabinet: first the door with no overlay, then a white person icon inside a semi-transparent dark rectangle with rounded corners, then the icon changes to a person with a raised hand accompanied by a short, centered three-line phrase about creatures that see and react to the audience (sheet 0007, q0058 to q0060).
- The initial credits screen is a state change of the same scene, not a cut: the door with two floating circular icons starts to display the experience's title in uppercase, centered over the panels, with slight letter spacing (sheet 0004, q0032 to q0034).
- The difference between frames documents the portal's choreography: three practically identical frames with the dinosaur still, then the empty portal, then the animal reappears more centered and, in the next frame, the whole scene loses saturation and contrast until it is almost white, signaling a fade out (sheet 0012, q0100 to q0106).
- The interaction gestures appear in first person, with no overlaid interface: an open-palm hand approaching the baby dinosaur, then extended fingers touching the animal's head, and later the adult dinosaur turning its head to face the camera (sheet 0009, q0074, q0076, q0079 and q0080).
- The behavior branching, which the narration describes in words, is drawn as a color-coded flowchart: gray boxes for the fixed entry and exit states, green boxes for the interaction steps, thin black arrows. The high point is the transformation between frames, when the linear sequence of four boxes turns into a grid of six parallel options with a light blue outline, including two choices that did not exist before in the line (sheet 0013, q0112 to q0117).
- The degree of audience involvement becomes a quantified graphic: the same isometric portal diagram gains a bar below it with thumbnails of real scenes linked by thin blue arrows, and the number of thumbnails grows from zero to four, five and six as the label changes from "Little" to "Some" and "Lots of audience interaction", ending with two stacked thumbnails that represent alternative outcomes (sheet 0014, q0121 to q0126).
- The emotional journey is a staircase of bars with increasing and decreasing height, with the reached step in dark gray, the following ones in light gray and a facial expression emoji above each step, labeled as build-up, action, relief, action and satisfaction. In a second pass, the generic blocks are replaced by real product photos without changing the staircase structure (sheet 0015, q0128 to q0132).
- The alternative interaction system appears as a concrete interface, not as a concept: a dark, semi-transparent card with rounded corners anchored to the floor of the real environment saying that no actions are available, which expands into a menu with a question and two stacked rectangular buttons, "Be Friendly" and "Be Forceful", and then returns to the collapsed state (sheet 0016, q0141 to q0144).
- The same summary pattern repeats at distant points in the video: horizontal rows of three or four simple black pictograms with a short caption below, identical in style, used for cinema, games and theater in the opening, for the four interaction principles, for the two functions of spatial audio and for the four accessibility features (sheets 0001 q0004, 0011, 0012 q0107 and 0015 q0135).
- Typographic intertitles separate the blocks: short phrases in large black type, centered over a plain white background, with no other graphic element, among them "Everything changes in device." (sheets 0006, 0008, 0010 and 0011).
- The visual recap exists as its own screen: under the title about maintaining immersion, three real photos in thumbnail form of the same size and straight edges in a row, revisiting the three touch moments already shown, first just the title and then the title with the photos (sheet 0011, q0094 to q0095).

Visual proportion: based on the notes, most of the frames are occupied by diagrams, menu screens, intertitles and experience scenes, with the presenters appearing intermittently in almost all the sheets and occupying only the final closing sheet on their own (sheet 0017).
Recorded divergences or limits: one of the text cards appears with such low opacity that it becomes almost illegible against the white background, probably caught in the middle of a fade (sheet 0011, q0092). The notes leave two identifications open, the second floating icon over the door, described as looking like a skull or the app's logo (sheet 0004, q0032), and the small recurring white dinosaur over the studio furniture, recorded as a probable set prop with no connection to the narration (sheets 0002, 0004, 0006, 0016 and 0017).
