### What the images show
Basis: 31 of 31 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.

- The screen construction happens live inside Keynote, and the frames show the layer order: a vertical white rectangle in the center of the canvas as the device frame (sheet 0010, q0086), then the blue header bar (sheet 0011, q0094 and q0095), then the divider lines (sheet 0012, q0101 to q0107), then the text (sheet 0013, q0115 and q0116) and finally the cropped photo distributed in the list (sheet 0015, q0128 to q0132; sheet 0016, q0137 to q0142). The Keynote side panel appears open with Fill, Border, Shadow, Reflection and Opacity, that is, a common vector editor playing the role of a UI tool (sheet 0010, q0083 to q0085).
- A screenshot of the real Messages app sits right next to the design under construction during almost the entire demonstration, serving as a ruler for scale, color and typography (sheet 0011, q0092 to q0099; sheet 0012, q0102 and q0106; sheet 0014, q0120 to q0122; sheet 0016, q0140). The reference only disappears once the mockup is already finished (sheet 0016, q0142).
- Measurement labels appear overlaid on the video during the drags, showing numbers that the speech does not state: "376 pt x 103 pt" on the header bar, "x: 376 pt y: 632 pt" repeated over lines and cursor, "x: 100 pt y: 75 pt" on the blue square and "Square: 94..." during the mask resizing (sheet 0011, q0096 and q0099; sheet 0012, q0105; sheet 0014, q0121; sheet 0015, q0130).
- The keyboard shortcuts used are captioned at the bottom of the frame as they go, working as a didactic annotation over the recording: ⌘A (sheet 0011, q0091), ⌘C and ⌘V in the copy frames (sheet 0012, q0101; sheet 0013, q0110 and q0111), ⌥⌘= and ⌥⌘- in the distribution and alignment frames (sheet 0011, q0097; sheet 0012, q0103 and the sheet's other frames) and ⌃⇧⌘4 at the moment of the area capture (sheet 0016, q0136).
- The same list assembly method appears twice, which shows that it is standard and not an isolated trick: duplicate the element several times loosely, fix only the last copy, and then select everything to distribute and align. First with simple divider lines (sheet 0012, q0101 to q0107), then with the whole item block, photo plus title plus description (sheet 0016, q0137 to q0141).
- The image crop is done with an overlaid shape serving as a mask, with the selection handles visible at the corners and the photo being trimmed until it becomes a perfect square (sheet 0015, q0128 to q0132). The same technique extracts only the status bar from a screenshot, clock and battery, instead of redesigning it (sheet 0015, q0131).
- The typography is fixed by direct visual comparison with the reference alongside it: the test text is typed over the mockup and appears first incomplete and then complete and selected, with the text box handles visible (sheet 0013, q0115 and q0116). The system color wheel and the predefined style swatches appear open in the side panel in these same frames (sheet 0013, q0109, q0112 and q0117).
- The content fidelity evolves on screen: it starts with a blue square, a "Text" box with a diagonal line and a single dish photo (sheet 0010, q0085), goes through the same dish duplicated four times and then filling the whole column (sheet 0016, q0138 and q0139), and reaches eight different dishes, each with a photo, bold name and its own description (sheet 0016, q0142).
- The slides use their own stage diagram system that the speech does not describe: clouds of gray dots for the raw list of ideas, green for what matters to the customer and their goals, blue for the app's goals, and a uniform 3x3 orange grid for the organized stage (sheet 0003, q0026 and q0027; sheet 0004, q0032 and q0036; sheet 0006, q0054; sheet 0007, q0063).
- The choice between options is shown by text weight and opacity, never by color, and the discarded alternative remains visible and faded instead of disappearing immediately. In the slide with questions about the customer, each line is half full white and half gray after the decision (sheet 0006, from q0048 to q0049), and the same grammar reappears in the talk's guiding questions (sheet 0003, q0023; sheet 0008, q0071).
- The scope selection is a two-beat movement on screen: first green checkmarks appear next to six items within the full feature list, and only afterward do the unmarked items fade until they almost disappear (sheet 0008, q0064 to q0067, then q0068 with the title changed and without the checks).
- The anatomy of the menu screen becomes legible in the prototypes: dish name in bold, description in gray below, price in green aligned to the right, small square photo to the right of the row, and a thin divider between items. The variant without a photo increases the size and weight of the dish name to fill the freed-up space (sheet 0018, q0158 to q0161).
- The variations run through a full spectrum of layout for the same content: text-only list, three-column grid with a caption below, two-column cards with text overlaid on the photo, edge-to-edge rectangular photo in a single column, and a photo dominating almost the entire screen (sheet 0019, q0163 to q0171).
- The critique is done inside a comparison grid of twelve menu screens side by side (sheet 0020, q0175), which in the following frames appears labeled A to L (sheet 0021, q0189). The elimination happens through loss of opacity column by column, leaving six (sheet 0022, q0190 to q0198), then three (sheet 0024, q0216; sheet 0025, q0220), then two (sheet 0025, q0221) and finally a diagonal blue "WINNER" stamp over option K (sheet 0026, q0229). The intermediate comparisons appear in pairs, B against C, G against H and J against K (sheets 0023 and 0024, q0199 to q0215).
- Component states are shown literally before and after the tap: the footer button goes from green "Add to Cart" to gray "Added to Cart", and the bag icon appears in the header next to the title (sheet 0027, q0243; sheet 0028, q0244 to q0245 and q0246).
- The purchase flow is drawn as graphic notation of screens linked by arrows, and the three versions appear in sequence with the count changing within the image itself: three screens, then four with "Order Summary" inserted, then three again with "Entree Details" removed (sheet 0027, q0239 to q0242; sheet 0028, q0250 to q0252; sheet 0029, q0259 and q0260). In the final version, a tray fixed at the bottom of the menu screen shows the price and an "ORDER" button and then reuses the same area to become a status card with a checkmark and estimated time (sheet 0029, q0255 to q0258).

Visual proportion: the 31 sheets are dominated by on-screen content. Text and diagram slides concentrate in sheets 0001 to 0009, reappear in sheets 0020, 0021, 0026 and 0030 and close the talk in sheet 0031; the Keynote recording occupies sheets 0010 to 0017; screen prototypes, comparison grids and flow diagrams occupy sheets 0018 to 0029. Frames of the presenter alone with no graphic appear interspersed in almost every sheet, almost always in short sequences of two to four frames.

Recorded divergences or limits: the on-screen labels mark 376 pt of width while the speech cites 375 by 667 points, and the notes record the frame as approximately 376x667 (sheet 0010; sheet 0011, q0096). The speech announces eleven alternatives, but the grid shown has twelve cells labeled A through L, with the notes recording that one of them was not described (sheet 0021, q0189). Several texts were partially cut off or illegible in the frames: the sentence about the user in q0044, the word starting with "Fee" in q0069, the value of the square in q0130 and the voting slide next to option K, too small to read (sheet 0025, q0224 and q0225). The colored icon on the stage pedestal also did not allow detail to be read (sheet 0009, q0079).
