### What the images show
Basis: 44 of 44 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.

- The anatomy of an iOS screen is taught in a mockup where all the content is placeholder text: navigation bar with a back button "< Parent View" and centered title, grouped table with an uppercase header, repeated cells labeled "Table View Cell Label" with a disclosure arrow, and a bottom tab bar with four generically shaped icons (star, circle, square, triangle). The list of the five wayfinding questions appears overlaid on this capture in one frame; in the following ones, the content switches to descriptive labels under the header "OTHER PLACES YOU CAN GO" and the back button changes to "< Exit" (sheet 0008, q0067 to q0072).
- The structure of the slides is a sequence of isolated concepts: a white word or phrase, centered, filling the entire screen before any example ("Human", "Humanity", "Design Principles", "Feedback", "Visibility", "Consistency", "Mental Model", "Mapping", "Progressive Disclosure", "Symmetry"), almost always on a dark background, with the exception of "Symmetry", which enters on a solid blue background. The opening title slide uses a simple left-aligned grid with hierarchy by size, small label at the top, large title, smaller subtitle (sheets 0001, 0004, 0010, 0016, 0017, 0022, 0030, 0036, 0039).
- The lists grow item by item at the pace of the speech, and the item under discussion is marked by weight and color: on sheet 0003 the list "Everyone Needs to..." goes from one to three items and the list "Apps Should Provide..." from one to four (q0019 to q0026), and the first one reappears complete, with four needs, on the closing slide (sheet 0042, q0378); in the list of feedback types the active item is white and bold while the other three are grayed out, going from "Status" to "Completion" and then "Errors" (sheets 0010, 0012 and 0013, q0086 to q0088, q0101, q0117).
- An explicit right-and-wrong pair in icon choice: the two share symbols appear first isolated one per screen, the arrow coming out of a box and the three connected dots (sheet 0019, q0167 and q0168), and then side by side in the same slide with a green checkmark under one and a pink X under the other (sheet 0020, q0172).
- Progressive disclosure is demonstrated with the macOS print dialog inside Keynote, in two distinct states separated by a frame where the presenter appears overlaid: first just printer, copies and pages, then the expanded dialog with presets, slide layout thumbnails and advanced option boxes (sheet 0038, q0336 to q0339).
- The fictional faucet travels the path from technical drawing to interface icon within a single sheet: blueprint on a beige background with a millimeter grid, thin lines, curved rotation arrows and uppercase labels ("HOT", "WATER FLOW"); reduction to three white icons on black; and finally colored arcs as functional code, red and blue for temperature, gray for flow (sheet 0025, q0217 to q0223).
- The mismatch between expectation and behavior gets its own graphic symbol, a red star with a "≠" sign placed between pairs of controls, which moves from one pair to the other between frames (sheet 0025, q0220 and q0221).
- Screenshots of real apps serve as proof of the interface's exact state, not as illustration: Mail with blue unread dots and the list reordering with the "Updated Just Now" label; Calendar on the event details screen with the guest highlighted in blue and a "Delete Event" button at the end; Camera recording, with the timer stopped at "00:00:13" while the button changes from a circle to a filled red square; Messages with an Apple Pay payment bubble for "$28" (sheets 0011 and 0013, q0095 to q0099, q0109, q0110, q0113).
- Inline validation and autocorrection are shown step by step: a "Sign Up" form with a checkmark on valid fields and a red icon plus an error message under the problematic field, followed by a date picker in search format where the typed text goes from "6/" to "6/31" and the suggestion list returns dates from July 1st (sheet 0014, q0120 to q0124).
- The conversation metaphor is reenacted in interface form: the same list of four feedback questions, previously in plain text, is redesigned as stacked chat app bubbles, with a blue reply bubble simulating the designer responding (sheet 0015, q0127 to q0129).
- Mapping is demonstrated through overlaid spatial correspondence: the room's floor plan in thin white lines, with yellow dots at the light fixture positions (sheet 0027, q0239; sheet 0030, q0267), receives the set of three switches aligned horizontally in positional correspondence with the lights, and then that set is left alone on the screen (sheet 0030, q0268 and q0269). In another frame, four identical switches receive the same "Light" label above each one, making it visible that they belong to the same class (sheet 0027, q0243).
- Affordance is built through comparison on the same object and through annotation on a prototype: the 3D plate in pastel tones rotates at various angles, receives a cup in one frame and a hamburger in the next (sheet 0032, q0283 to q0285; sheet 0033, q0293 and q0294); next, a MacBook keyboard mockup carries the annotation "Put finger here to lift screen" and, in the following frame, a horizontal slider drawn within the trackpad area (sheet 0034, q0300 and q0301).
- Axes of symmetry are manually marked over real screenshots: a vertical dotted line at the center of the Weather, Camera in selfie mode and the Phone numeric keypad screens, and dashed horizontal lines separating items in the World Clock and Weather lists to highlight the uniform repetition (sheet 0041, q0363 to q0369). Before that, the three types of symmetry are taught with three pairs of white triangles side by side, each labeled with its type, and then applied to a photo of a coral reef with a switch icon overlaid (sheet 0040, q0352 to q0359).
- The working tools appear on screen: the Keynote window with a slide thumbnail panel on the left, canvas in the center and a text formatting panel on the right (font, styles, alignment, spacing), and the Sketch file with a layers panel containing pages, color and font guides, templates and UI elements, dozens of artboards in a grid in the center and a properties panel on the right with position, transform, radius, opacity, blending, borders and shadows (sheet 0029, q0253 to q0258).
- The summary slide is built up by accumulation and receives its final argument through typography and position: the map of principles grouped by trip-stage icon (plane, car, hotel, hamburger, fish) appears incomplete and then complete, and in a later frame the word "Human" is inserted at a larger size in the central space that was previously empty between the groups (sheet 0042, q0374 and q0375; sheet 0043, q0387).

Visual proportion: by the notes, most of the frames are slides, flat illustrations and narrative emojis, with the presenter alone or overlaid in translucency in part of almost every sheet; captures of Apple apps and macOS tools are concentrated in about nine of the 44 sheets (0011, 0013, 0017, 0028, 0029, 0035, 0038, 0041, 0042), and in three others the interface is a generic mockup or fictional app (0008, 0014, 0018). The notes do not record any live demonstration, only screen captures.

Recorded divergences or limits: several sequences of frames are noted as practically identical, with no perceptible change, among them the faucet blueprint (q0217 to q0219), the Sketch screen (q0256 to q0258), the cheeseburger options table (q0321 and q0322) and the three types of symmetry (q0353 and q0354); and a frame of the Phone dialer appears in a schematic version with blurred text blocks (sheet 0041, q0367).
