### What the images show
Basis: 14 of 14 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.
- Anatomy of the "More" button: translucent circular container with an ellipsis glyph overlaid on the bottom right corner of the screen, shown first over the Maps map (sheet 0006, q0052) and then in the Camera viewfinder, where it pairs with the timer button at the bottom (sheet 0007, q0061 and q0063; sheet 0008, q0065 to q0067, this last one zoomed in on the pair of buttons). The captures show the same component reused in different contexts, something the speech only lists.
- What the button opens: a modal menu occupying the bottom strip of the screen with stacked options and "Cancel" at the top in gray (sheet 0006, q0053 and q0054), then the same menu isolated over a black background (sheet 0007, q0057).
- Intermediate state of the swipe gesture: the World Clock list appears with the Shanghai row partially displaced and the red rectangular delete button emerging underneath (sheet 0005, from q0044 to q0045), a frame that records the middle of the gesture, not just the result.
- Frame difference that reveals the toolbar button: the same Messages list without the blue pill (sheet 0010, q0088) and, in the next frame, with the full width blue pill at the top pushing the list down (q0089). The two frames record the before and after of the state; the scroll gesture that produces the reveal is left to the speech.
- Code on screen next to the result: SwiftUI block in white monospaced font over dark gray, with keywords in pink and purple and strings in orange, placed side by side with the Watch mockup (sheet 0011, q0094 to q0096), linking the toolbar API to the visible blue button.
- Stocks view switcher: same list of indices in "Viewing: Percentage" with the variation in green (sheet 0005, q0037) and then in "Viewing: Points" with the same indices in absolute values (q0041), with the current mode written in gray right below the app title.
- List sorting in Activity Sharing: "Sort By" header with the active criterion highlighted, list with concentric activity rings on the left, name, numeric value and progress bar, and dot pagination at the bottom; changing the criterion changes the listed names and the displayed metric, from percentage and calories in sorting by name to minutes and percentage in sorting by exercise (sheet 0004, from q0030 to q0034).
- Contrast in treatment between informative and actionable content: in the event detail, the note sits directly over a black background with no container, while the destructive action sits inside a dark rectangular container with red text (sheet 0010, q0084), a difference in material and color that the speech does not detail.
- Hierarchical navigation seen in the mechanism: the bar title changes depending on the level (single inbox, then list of accounts, then the chosen account) with the orange back arrow always to the left of the title, first in Mail and then in the Home app with the list of houses (sheet 0012, q0100 to q0103 and q0106 to q0108).
- Hierarchy by weight and color on a choice screen: in the response to the Calendar invite, the accepted option appears in green with a selection mark and the rest in gray, with the button to contact the sender separated below the list (sheet 0009, from q0080 to q0081); in the Camera settings list, the active option is marked with a check on the right (sheet 0008, q0064).
- Color as an action signal per app: blue highlight for the new message and purple for the new note (sheet 0002, q0012 and q0016), full width orange rectangle at the bottom to add a city (sheet 0006), red reserved for destructive actions (sheets 0005 and 0010).
- Persistent visual summary: the list of topics always stays in the same right corner, with the item under discussion in white and bold and the rest dimmed in gray over the blurred video, appearing item by item (sheet 0003, q0026 and q0027) and reappearing with a different item in focus at each block (sheets 0004 to 0010), which makes it the visible structure of the talk itself.
- Text cards over a black background marking each principle: at the opening the card comes centered and alone (sheet 0001, q0009); then the same type of sentence appears next to the Watch mockup, first on the left of the black screen and in the next frame on the right, with the watch occupying the other side (sheet 0002, q0011 and q0012). The credits screen brings the resources in two columns, name on the left and source in gray on the right (sheet 0013, q0114).
- Juxtaposition of two watches side by side as a recurring comparison device, used to put one app screen next to another, next to a block of code or next to a second watch with a face photo (sheets 0002, 0011 and 0013, for example q0016, q0096 and q0112), showing the same pattern across different apps.
Visual proportion: sheets 0002 to 0012 are dominated by Watch screen mockups, with the presenter interspersed between examples; sheet 0013 mixes this watch comparison with the credits screen, and the opening and closing (sheets 0001 and 0014) are set design, talking head and text card, with no app interface.
