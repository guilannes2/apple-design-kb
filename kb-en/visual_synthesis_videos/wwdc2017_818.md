### What the images show
Basis: 10 of 10 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.
- The construction of the prototype inside Keynote is visible step by step, in a document with iPhone screen proportions: a solid blue circle gives way to a larger light gray circle serving as a dial, which gains a vertical blue bar and a green start button, and finally the bar is swapped for a thin red line acting as the pointer (sheet 0004, q0032 to q0036). The speech describes the process; the screen shows which panel is open at each step, with color, border, shadow and opacity samples on the right.
- The animation is also shown as an actual configuration, not as a concept: a rotation panel with duration, clockwise direction, angle and acceleration, and a fade-out panel, with the build order list alongside (sheet 0005, q0037 and q0038); three red strokes fanned out represent intermediate positions of the pointer and give way to a single vertical stroke when the rotation ends.
- A numeric counter overlaid on the recording works as a countdown timer for the demonstration itself, falling from sixty to twenty-seven during the assembly and then from fifteen to values with decimal places in the animation phase (sheet 0004, q0032 to q0036; sheet 0005, q0037 to q0039). This feature exists only in the image.
- The before and after of the iteration appears on distinct screens: the light gray dial with a thin red pointer (sheet 0005) turns into a solid red circle with a white pointer for contrast (sheet 0008, q0065), a change in color hierarchy that the speech sums up in two words.
- The action button also changes nature between frames, from a labeled green box with defined borders to just the text with no colored background, occupying the entire lower width of the screen, with the side panel changing from shape style to text style (sheet 0008, q0065 to q0066). It is the principle of the hit target being larger than the visible element, shown and not only stated.
- A completion screen is created in front of the viewer, with a blank slide receiving the closing text in red over a light background, in the same palette as the timer, preceded by the transition effects panel (sheet 0008, q0067 to q0068).
- The tests happen in a real filmed context, not in a presentation setting: the iPhone with the prototype on the white table next to a beaker and test tube receiving drops of reagent, with the pointer advancing between frames (sheet 0006, q0046 to q0047), and then next to hands solving a Rubik's cube, with the pointer at progressively different angles (q0049 to q0053).
- The live demonstration carries an overlaid caption warning that this is not a real app, over the video of the prototype in hand (sheet 0002, q0016), and the prototype itself has no status bar, numbers or icons, only a circle, pointer and button (sheets 0002 and 0006).
- The test's learning becomes a slide with explicit typographic hierarchy: first two large centered questions and then a list with four main questions in white and smaller secondary questions in gray under each one, filling the entire slide (sheet 0007, q0060 to q0062).
- Process diagrams use few shapes and arrows, with no icons or photos: the cycle of three arrows with make, show and learn (sheet 0003, q0024 and q0026; sheet 0007), three loose words that gain a curved arrow linking two of them (sheet 0009, q0074 to q0075) and four circles in shades of blue linked by a straight line that then receive additional curved arrows forming a network (q0076 to q0077).
- A grid of nine photographs of different prototypes in hands, each with its own screen, palette and purpose, shows the variety that the speech only mentions in a generic way (sheet 0008, q0070 to q0071).
- The same messaging conversation frames the narrative: it appears in the opening with the request for the app and in the close with the reply sent, a blue bubble containing a video thumbnail of the prototype with a play button (sheet 0001, q0007 and q0008; sheet 0010, q0085 to q0086).
- The close uses a slide of pairs in two columns, question in gray on the left and advice in white on the right, with the third line entering only in the next frame (sheet 0010, q0082 to q0083).
Visual proportion: most of the video is screen recording of Keynote and test video with the prototype on a physical device, complemented by short text and diagram slides, with the presenter alone on screen during transitions.
