### What the images show
Basis: 13 of 13 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.

- The physical set already anticipates the theme: three-dimensional "A" letters of different weights and widths sit on the counter behind the presenter, in the studio shot that opens the talk, before any slide (sheet 0001).
- The agenda slides sit on a light background, in a vertical list with no bullets or numbering, with the active item in bold dark text and the upcoming items in light gray (sheet 0001, q0006 to q0007); the same list reappears in sheet 0003, where the highlighted item stays the same and the faded item below it disappears (q0025 to q0026).
- The SF family is presented by moving through a static list over a black background (SF Pro, Pro Rounded, Compact, Compact Rounded, Mono), each name lighting up in white while the others dim, with Compact and Compact Rounded lit together in the same frame (sheet 0002, q0010 to q0012); in the same sheet, the weights turn into a row of lowercase "a"s growing in weight from left to right with the weight name below each letter, and the optical sizes turn into stacked blocks marked 96, 70 and 45 pts on the left (q0016 to q0017).
- The new axis is taught as a matrix: rows labeled Compressed, Condensed, Regular and Expanded, columns of increasing weight from Ultralight to Black, and one row lit at a time while the others stay gray (sheet 0004, q0028, q0031, q0033).
- The space savings is proven by controlled repetition: the same paragraph with European language names appears twice, one labeled Compressed and the other Expanded, changing only the font width and, with it, the number of lines (sheet 0004, q0034 to q0035).
- The anatomy of the letter is isolated in four stages: the word "on" in the four styles with no annotation, then with a horizontal dotted line measuring the width of each one, then with beige color bands overlaid on the inner curves, and finally reduced to abstract teardrop shapes with no text at all, showing only the counter-form (sheet 0005, q0037 to q0040).
- The pangram "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog" turns into a four-row table, one per width, with a rotating highlight and then all rows lit at the same time, making visible how much horizontal space each style consumes for the same content (sheet 0005, q0041 to q0044).
- The point-size compensation is shown numerically: all lines at 20 pts with different final widths, and then the sizes adjusted per style, from 42 pts in Compressed to 20 pts in Expanded, so that all fit the same width (sheet 0006, q0049 to q0050).
- There is explicit signaling of what to avoid: four columns of running paragraph text side by side, one per style, and a yellow warning triangle appears first over the Compressed column and then also over the Condensed one, marking the long text that loses legibility (sheet 0006, q0052 to q0054).
- The anatomy of the Photos Memory card is annotated with a callout line connecting the label "Title" to the text "PET FRIENDS"; next, the same title is tested in pairs of styles side by side, with the weight and width name next to each sample (q0058 to q0060), up to a 3x3 grid with nine combinations of the same title in two lines (q0061), all in sheet 0007.
- The News headline receives callout lines marking which part uses Compressed and which uses Expanded, and the same composition is repeated on three different backgrounds, light beige, white and dark gray, keeping the annotation (sheet 0008, q0064 to q0066).
- The Maps typographic system appears as a list of six categories in different sizes, each row labeled on the left in yellow with the weight and width pair used, from LANDMARK in Regular Semibold to MOUNTAIN in Expanded Bold (sheet 0008, q0067 to q0069).
- The recap is built by accumulation, with a circular green check icon entering item by item while the previous ones remain visible (sheet 0009, q0074 to q0078).
- Consistency across writing systems is demonstrated by a side-by-side comparison of the same system screen in three languages, Settings, home screen, shortcuts and keyboard in Spanish, English and Chinese, with identical component structure and only the text changing (sheet 0010, q0082 to q0089), and by lock screens that display the time in different numbering systems over distinct color gradients (sheet 0011, q0092 to q0093).
- Arabic receives the same metric treatment given earlier to Latin: thin vertical guide lines measuring the word at 96 pts and then at 15 pts (sheet 0012, q0101 and q0102), an optical size table from 96 to 14 pts, language columns separated by writing system (q0104 to q0105) and a grid of isolated Arabic glyphs with small variations in shape and marks, all in the same sheet 0012; the closing brings six Arabic-language app screens in dark mode and the developer.apple.com/fonts page with "Aa" sample cards (sheet 0013, q0110 to q0114).

Visual proportion: based on the notes, almost all the screen time is taken up by typographic slides, sample tables and app captures, with the presenter alone appearing in short transition segments in sheets 0001, 0003, 0009, 0011 and 0013.

Recorded divergences or limits: in sheet 0008, the notes describe the Maps category list once as descending size order and once as going from small LANDMARK to large MOUNTAIN, so the direction of the scale is left open; in sheet 0004, the multilingual block labeled Compressed is called the widest style in the screen description and the most compact text in the frame comparison, and here the second reading holds, which is the one compared frame by frame.
