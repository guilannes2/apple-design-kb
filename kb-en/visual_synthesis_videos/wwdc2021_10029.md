# wwdc2021_10029: Design for Safari 15

### What the images show
Basis: 27 of 27 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.

- Two fixed settings separate the roles without it being stated: the presenter speaks about concept at a light-colored desk with an orange iMac facing away, books and two toy figures (sheets 0001 to 0007, 0009, 0011 to 0014 and 0016), and the second presenter demonstrates code in a distinct setting, described in sheet 0007 with a dark-screen Mac, a rubber duck and a plant, and reappearing in sheets 0008 to 0010 and 0014 to 0016. The change of setting is the visual marker of the shift from concept to demonstration.
- The dominant teaching pattern is source code and rendered result side by side, with the line under discussion highlighted in a lighter background box, gray in most frames and blue in some, which changes position from frame to frame as the explanation advances (sheets 0003, 0004, 0007 to 0010, 0012 to 0016, 0018, 0021 to 0025). The order of the sides varies: in sheet 0003 the code is on the left and the browser on the right, and in sheets 0008 to 0010 it is the opposite. Only the image locates which line was in focus at each moment.
- The thesis of the disappearing frame is staged literally: the 1990s browser window, with File, Options, Navigate menus and a URL field, keeps losing content and contrast until only an almost empty gray rectangle with a narrow bar at the top remains (sheet 0002, q0010 to q0012).
- theme-color is shown by its immediate effect on the bar: the code gains the second meta tag with the media attribute for light and dark and the browser's tab bar changes from yellow to green in the same pair of frames (sheet 0003, q0021 to q0023), and then two windows appear stacked in reversed order for a direct comparison of the two colors (sheet 0003, q0026 to q0027).
- In the Dog Dog DC example, the code and the result advance together: first the line `<meta name="theme-color" content="#0b85ff">` is added and highlighted next to the blue bar already visible (sheet 0008, q0071 to q0072); then the gray newsletter strip disappears between one frame and the next and the blue starts to fill uninterrupted from the top of the window down to the content, with the layout variables CSS shown alongside (sheet 0009, q0075 to q0077 to q0081).
- The dynamic color change by state is shown as code and layout simultaneously: the editor shows `toggleTheaterMode()` manipulating classList and toggling the meta's content between "#0b85ff" and "#0e0e0e", while the interface alongside moves from the normal state to Theater Mode with the stream list compacted (sheet 0010, q0085 to q0087).
- The safe area variables get a specification diagram: four labels connected by thin lines to the four edges of an iPhone tinted green, naming inset top, left, right and bottom (sheet 0013, q0111), plus leader lines labeled "Safe area" and `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` pointing to the green strip between the end of the content and the bottom edge (sheet 0012, q0103 and q0105).
- The dynamic nature of the measurement is converted into a visual gradation: three iPhones side by side with the same screen covered by a green layer of increasing opacity from left to right (sheet 0012, q0102), a technique that the speech expresses only in words.
- Before and after rotation: the two iPhones side by side rotate from portrait to landscape through intermediate frames with motion blur, instead of a hard cut (sheet 0013, q0112 to q0113), and the article text goes from flush against and partly cut off by the rounded left edge to indented and legible within the margin (sheet 0013, q0115 to q0117).
- The effect of `viewport-fit=cover` is shown by the extension of the color: the site's purple header, which previously stopped at a light margin, now reaches the rounded edges of the device, with the viewport attribute changed and highlighted in the code and leader lines pointing to the side insets (sheet 0014, q0119 to q0121).
- The iPhone's bottom tab bar appears following the theme color of each site, goes from expanded to minimized showing only the domain, and gives way to the grid of overlapping tab thumbnails (sheet 0011, q0093 to q0098).
- The tab title is taught by comparing two versions of the same code, with the recommended pattern, content title before the site name, highlighted in a gray box next to a documentation page in the MDN style about CSS Grid Layout (sheet 0007, q0055 to q0056).
- The rich link is built line by line on screen: the HTML block gains og:title, then og:image and finally og:video, each new line highlighted, and the card on the phone alongside gains a title, an image and finally a play icon over the image (sheet 0018, q0157 to q0161).
- aspect-ratio is explained in three different registers: blue measurement rulers with double arrows marking 800px and 400px over the photo of the dog (sheet 0021, q0183 to q0184); a text card that turns into a square when it receives `aspect-ratio: 1/1` and then grows in height to fit the content, appearing at the end in four different widths (sheet 0022, q0194 to q0199); and geometric diagrams of a square and a 2x2 grid with lists of equivalent declarations, followed by two columns with ratios for cinema, TV, paper and art (sheet 0023, q0202 to q0207).

- The set of redesigned native controls is shown as a comparative grid of white cards labeled one by one (Checkboxes, Date and time inputs, Range, Radio buttons, Standard buttons, File upload, Color, Progress, Meter) over a light gray background (sheet 0025, q0224 to q0225), preceded by the calendar card with a blue header, a grid of days and the selected day highlighted (sheet 0025, q0222 to q0223).
