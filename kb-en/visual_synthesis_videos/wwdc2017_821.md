### What the images show
Basis: 9 of 9 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.
- The central concept gets its own diagram, an organic shield-like shape with a continuous gradient from green to magenta and a black outline overlaid marking the smaller space within the larger one, reused literally from the definition slide to the conclusion slide (sheet 0002, q0011 to q0016; sheet 0009, q0076 and q0077). It works as the talk's visual signature.
- Color management is explained by a flow diagram that builds in two stages: two photos of the same boy with balloons labeled "Original Asset" and "Customers' Device" connected by an arrow, and then a third central box with a tool icon entering between them (sheet 0001, q0008 to q0009).
- Color depth comparison made with red gradient bars of the same width stacked one by one, labeled sRGB with 8 bits per channel, sRGB with 8 bits plus dithering and Display P3 with 16 bits per channel (sheet 0003, q0020 to q0022). The image itself is the argument about banding.
- Actual Photoshop screens show the exact location of each field that the talk sums up in verbal instruction: the "New" dialog with Color Mode set to RGB and 16 bit and the "Advanced" section expanded with Color Profile set to Display P3 (sheet 0006, q0046 to q0047); the "Save As" dialog with the file in PNG and the embed color profile checkbox checked at the bottom (q0054); Camera Raw with photo and histogram (sheet 0007).
- Right-and-wrong pair marked by annotation on the capture itself: the "Convert to Profile" dialog, with Source Space, Destination Space set to Display P3, Engine Adobe ACE, Intent Relative Colorimetric and auxiliary checkboxes, is followed by the "Assign Profile" dialog covered by a red circular X of disapproval (sheet 0007, q0061 to q0062). The talk only says not to use it; the X convention is purely visual.
- Gamut comparison made by splitting the same image in half vertically, each half labeled in white text at the bottom, with the pair of macaws visibly more vibrant on the Display P3 side, and the dividing line shifting between frames (sheet 0005, q0040 to q0041). Before that the same photo appears with blue patches marking what the smaller space can't represent and then in an exaggerated-saturation version (q0038 to q0039).
- Numeric effect of the conversion shown in Photoshop's Color Picker, with the circular selector shifting within the gradient square while the RGB values change from 255, 0, 0 to 236, 0, 0 and then 234, 51, 35 (sheet 0008, q0064 to q0066).
- Topic slides use highlight progression, the item under discussion in white and the rest grayed out, a mechanism repeated in the definition slide (sheet 0002), in the Photoshop workflow panel (sheet 0005, q0045; sheets 0006 and 0007) and in the conclusion slide (sheet 0009). The arrangement of a list on the left plus a gamut diagram on the right belongs only to sheets 0002 and 0009; in the workflow sheets the list appears alone, overlaid on the screen captures. This tracking mechanism is not described by the talk.
- The range of compatible devices appears as simple silhouettes displaying the same image: first an iMac alone with a mountain landscape, then iMac, iPhone and MacBook Pro side by side with the same flower photo (sheet 0004, q0029 to q0030), conveying color consistency across screens.
- End-use examples always appear inside an iPhone frame, never as a standalone image: a music app with a list of covers, a fixed bottom player, a colored waveform, BPM and sync buttons (sheet 0008, q0069 to q0070, before the crossfade that leads to the aurora photo in q0071 and q0072), and a fashion photo with a yellow dress on a magenta background used for true-to-life fabric color, overlaid by a second semi-transparent copy to compare versions (sheet 0009, q0073 to q0074).
- A square app icon with rounded corners with a diagonal red-to-yellow gradient over purple isolated on a dark screen illustrates the gain in gradient smoothness (sheet 0007, q0063).
- The display condition itself becomes a slide: a card warning that the presentation is being projected on an sRGB system precedes the question about who can see Display P3 images (sheet 0003, q0026 to q0027).
Visual proportion: conceptual slides and Photoshop screen captures predominate, with photos and device mockups in the color demonstrations, and the presenter alone in frame only in short passages between blocks.
Recorded divergences or limits: the notes record that the session's title slide carries "Session 807", a number different from the video's id.
