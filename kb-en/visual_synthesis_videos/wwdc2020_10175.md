### What the images show
Basis: 22 of 22 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.
- The screen works as a persistent summary: the list of four topics appears to the right of the presenter and the active item switches from full white bold to translucent gray as the speech advances, from "Optical sizes" to "Variable fonts" between two consecutive frames (sheet 0001, q0007 to q0009; sheet 0002, q0010 to q0011). The same pattern returns in the final recap, where each new principle enters in bold and the previous ones remain at a lighter weight, never disappearing (sheet 0021, q0187 to q0189; sheet 0022, q0190 to q0191).
- Typographic table with two columns named "SF Text" and "SF Display", with the same sample sentence repeated in sizes from 12 to 26 points and the active size row highlighted in white while the others stay gray; between frames the highlighted row goes from 18 to 20 points, following the progress of the size demonstration (sheet 0002, q0013 to q0014).
- Measurement annotation with no numbers, in two distinct forms: on sheet 0002 the word "aisle" appears twice with colored vertical bars marking the spacing between the letters, blue in the smaller scale version and orange in the larger one, and the two separate samples merge into a single block split down the middle, half labeled "SF Text" and half "SF Display" (q0015 to q0016); on sheet 0004 the marking changes to horizontal bars, one dark blue and one brown, placed like rulers next to the letter "i" to indicate the glyph's side spacing (q0029 to q0032).
- Historical evidence filmed, not illustrated: a close-up of hands leafing through an 18th-century copy with small-caps captions identifying the type size, followed by the pair of letters "a" with captions "8 points" and "42 points" and, right after, the same letters over a pixel grid where the version on the right appears clearly more grainy than the one on the left (sheet 0003, q0022 to q0027).
- Glyph anatomy shown in a font design tool: the letter "a" turns into a white vector outline with numbered nodes and curve handles, and then gains white arrows indicating the direction each point moves in, with the final solid shape overlaid, illustrating the interpolation mechanics of a variable font (sheet 0004, q0035 to q0036; sheet 0005, q0044 to q0045).
- A properties panel mockup in dark theme shows two columns of files (SFProText and SFProDisplay, eight weights each) converging by an arrow into a single "SFPro.ttf", with "Weight" and "Optical size" sliders overlaid; when the numeric field changes from 36 to 60 the slider handle moves right and the sample letter becomes wider and with a thicker stroke in the very next frame (sheet 0006, q0049 to q0054). The same panel displays a yellow warning triangle when the requested value, 96, falls outside the available range (sheet 0007, q0055).
- Formal data visualization for tracking: a line chart with a "Size (points)" axis and a "Tracking (1/2048em)" axis, blue and orange background areas by zone and circular markers on the curve. The curve evolves from a flat line at zero into a descending V, and then the labels change from "SF Text" and "SF Display" with a single cutoff at 20 to a single "SF Pro (variable)" label with two marked limits, 17 and 28, on the axis (sheet 0008, q0071 to q0072; sheet 0009, q0073 to q0075).
- A right-and-wrong pair signaled by icons, gray X and green checkmark, applied to the code and the result at the same time: swapping the kerning attribute for tracking changes the icon and, on the iPhone next to it, the text goes from truncated with an ellipsis to complete; next in the sequence the word "waffle" appears with the ligature preserved under the X and broken apart under the green checkmark (sheet 0009, q0079 to q0081; sheet 0010, q0082).
- The concepts of line height and leading get their own cards with literal illustration: a petrol blue rectangle wrapping a line to mark its height, orange vertical arrows measuring the space between two lines, and a photographic background of rusted metal type pieces as a reference to physical lead; the title changes from "Leading" to "Line-height includes leading" between frames (sheet 0011, q0091 to q0094).
- The Arabic case is proven on screen, not just stated: the word "Settings" in English and its Arabic version receive the same measurement arrows, and then two iPhone mockups show the Settings screen in Arabic with right-to-left reading (sheet 0011, q0097 to q0099).
- Real system screens serve as proof of each abstract concept: Calendar with the year in red in the large title style, Mail with the mailbox list, Fitness with large colored numeric values for high density, Maps with a more spaced-out place card, Book Store and Reminders, plus the music app with a label pointing to the "Browse" header identified as "Large Title" (sheet 0012, q0106 to q0108; sheet 0013, q0109 to q0112; sheet 0014, q0120 to q0122; sheet 0015, q0133).
- A leading comparison made through the difference between consecutive frames with the same placeholder text: the code gains the tight leading trait and the paragraph lines become visibly closer together, then switches to loose leading and the same paragraph opens up, with the line height values noted in a comment (sheet 0015, q0127 to q0129).
