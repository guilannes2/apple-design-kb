### What the images show
Basis: 35 of 35 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.
- The measurements appear annotated directly on the elements, in points, and are the video's main teaching device: the standard pointer's diameter marked as "19pt" next to the isolated gray circle, with a small vertical reference tick (sheet 0004, q0033); 12pt padding on all four sides of a blue button with a bezel and 24pt around a circular info icon with no bezel, in red annotations with arrows (sheet 0018); corner radii of 21pt, 15pt and 8pt overlaid in red on a pill and a rectangular button, in close zoom frames (sheet 0019, q0166 to q0168); and stroke thickness of 4.5pt versus 1pt on two crosses side by side (sheet 0025, q0219 to q0220).
- The anatomy of the Lift effect is shown as an exploded 3D diagram, with the layers stacked and named on screen: first just "Specular" and "Element", and in the next frame the same diagram gains "Radiosity" and "Shadow", meaning the layers are revealed progressively (sheet 0015, q0130 to q0131). Next the green message icon appears with the radiosity halo and, tilted, with a small gray circle next to it indicating the pointer's actual position (q0132 to q0133).
- Right-and-wrong pairs with a green check mark and a red X are the fixed teaching pattern, repeated across several contexts: pointer behind the element as approved versus pointer in front as rejected, in two schematic cards with a person icon connected by a line to a yellow square (sheet 0005); highlight background present in only two of three icons marked with an X and the version with all three backgrounds equal marked with a check (sheet 0017, q0146 to q0148); backgrounds of unequal sizes corrected with red alignment guide lines (q0149 to q0150); solid icon versus thin-outline icon (sheet 0025, q0217 to q0218).
- The hit region is drawn as a dashed frame larger than the icon's visible artwork, and the difference between frames shows the fit happening: the dashed outline first shifted up and to the left of the heart card, then coinciding exactly with the icon, illustrating the recentering (sheet 0010, q0082 to q0085). Two neighboring icons go from no visible frame to a single continuous dashed band with a shared gray background, with no gap between them (sheet 0018, q0160 to q0161).
- The magnetism algorithm gets its only direct graphic representation in the video: a spiral of dashed turquoise dots in growing concentric circles overlaid on the Home Screen, centered near an icon, and in the next frame the spiral disappears with the pointer already fixed on the Settings icon (sheet 0013, q0109 to q0110 to q0111).
- The precision problem is staged with screens built for it: the calendar grid appears empty, with thin horizontal lines and hour labels, with no visual mark of where each 15-minute block starts (sheet 0007), and only afterward does the purple event block labeled "2:15 PM" appear, with the arrow pointer just below it, confirming in the image the wrong rounding that the speech describes (sheet 0008, q0065).
- The same paragraph in serif font is shown again in successive selection states, including rotated diagonally with the highlight band following the tilt of the text, which shows that the highlight is attached to the line and not to the screen coordinates (sheet 0006, q0049 to q0051). In another frame the paragraph appears almost invisible, at very low opacity (q0046 to q0048).
- The two categories of pointer shape transformation are presented as a visual progression, from the rounded rectangle to the pill and then to the free-form star, and end with the two labels named on screen, "Rectangle" and "Arbitrary" (sheet 0023, q0199 to q0207); the next slide carries the signature of the two corresponding APIs on a black screen with syntax highlighting and a footer with the title "Build for the iPadOS pointer" and "WWDC20" (sheet 0024).
- The pointer's material is explained with a black-to-white gradient bar functioning as a contrast scale, with a circular marker and a red reference mark, and the marker changes position between frames, next to the "Mail" button shown with a gray background and then with no background (sheet 0022, q0191 to q0192).
- The anchor point is marked by a yellow dot on the shape: at the lower tip of the eyedropper icon, off the geometric center, and then at the exact center of a gray circle, comparing the two choices in consecutive frames (sheet 0026, q0232 to q0233).
- The applied effects appear as the difference between two frames of the same component: the blue "Continue" button with no highlight at all and, in the next frame, with a bluish glow halo around the edge (sheet 0016, q0137 to q0138); and the rounded gray highlight jumping from the share icon to the heart icon in an action bar, showing the successive fit (sheet 0009, q0077 to q0080 and q0081).
- Real system apps serve as case studies in the second half: a budget spreadsheet with the selection moving from one column to two adjacent ones while the footer button changes from "Cell" to "Column" (sheet 0029, q0253 to q0254), a text document with a thumbnail sidebar gaining a blue selection box with handles around an entire paragraph (sheet 0031, q0278 to q0279), calendar, Maps with the Apple Park card, and Control Center open over the Home Screen (sheets 0030, 0032 and 0033).
- A real photograph of a hand on a physical trackpad is inserted overlapping the corner of the iPad screen to illustrate the two-finger gesture, a mix of photo and mockup that the speech does not describe (sheet 0032, q0284 to q0285).
- The text slides use hierarchy by weight and opacity instead of bullet points: the session index has the active item in bold and the rest in light gray (sheets 0001 and 0013), and the recommendation lists reveal one item at a time, the current one in black and the previous ones faded in gray, aligned next to the presenter's face (sheet 0027, q0235 to q0239).
