# wwdc2021_10308: Accessibility by design: An Apple Watch for everyone

### What the images show
Basis: 14 of 14 frame sheets viewed, all codes checked.

- The video does not use a presentation slide or a code block on any sheet. The recorded format is documentary: chest-and-face shot interviews in a light-background studio with frosted glass panels and a wooden articulated mannequin sculpture on a circular pedestal, a scenic element that repeats from sheet to sheet (sheets 0001, 0002, 0004 to 0013) and serves as visual continuity between different interviewees.
- The opening and closing use the same pair of graphic devices: a mosaic of hundreds of face thumbnails with small light blue rounded rectangles and tiny overlaid icons, followed by a particle explosion as a transition (sheet 0001, q0001 to q0002), then an almost entirely black title card with left-aligned white sans-serif typography on two lines and an Apple Watch icon above the text (sheet 0001, q0003); the closing repeats the pattern with the white apple and "WWDC21" centered on a black screen (sheet 0014, q0123 to q0124).
- The watch faces shown function as a study in contrast and minimal element count per screen: high-contrast horizontal color bands filling the entire screen, with no visible numbers (sheet 0003, q0021), and a typographic watch face with the digits "9:41" in pure yellow on black filling almost the entire screen, with no other element (sheet 0004, q0034).
- Vertical hierarchy on a small screen appears explicit on the stocks screen: time at the top, index name in capital letters, large highlighted value, variation in green, thin line graph and "Market closed" status in light gray at the bottom, all on solid black (sheet 0003, q0026). The weather watch face repeats the logic in left-aligned vertical blocks, with city in blue, condition and high and low in white, and three small circular complications aligned at the bottom (sheet 0004, q0030).
- Messages on the wrist show the complete component: header with back arrow on the left and time on the right, contact name in bold, "To:" field below and body in a bubble with timestamp, with a finger touching the screen directly in both frames (sheet 0004, q0031 to q0032). Between one frame and the other the text scrolls and reveals different content from the same conversation.
- The Mickey Mouse watch face uses a classic analog layout with Arabic numerals around it, the character figure in place of the hands, and the moon phase in small capital letters at the bottom (sheet 0006, q0046). In every frame where it appears, the notice "Disney elements: © Disney" stays fixed in the bottom left corner of the watch screen (sheets 0006 to 0008), attribution data that exists only in the image.
- The repetition of the tap is staged through the juxtaposition of nearly identical frames: a row of five thumbnails of the same watch face with the finger in a slightly different position in each one (sheet 0007, q0058 to q0060), and then two fingers touching the Mickey area at the same time (sheet 0008, q0070).
- Gesture control is documented through a contrast in sharpness: in the earlier close-ups the hand appears sharp over the watch, and in the gesture frames the hand is blurred in motion while the screen reacts, moving from a simple watch face to a messages app menu (sheet 0011, q0097 to q0099). This menu shows "Cancel" fixed in blue in the top left corner, items with an icon on the left, and the quick suggestion "Hello!" as a reply option.
- The activation plus system feedback pair appears on two consecutive screens: an "Assistive Touch" settings list in the standard watchOS pattern, with an enabled toggle aligned to the right of the main item and a "Hand Gestures" submenu below (sheet 0012, q0104), and a "Double Pinch Recognized" notification card with bold title, explanatory body and a blue "Done" button in the corner (sheet 0012, q0105).
- The sequence of sheets stages the progression of the argument in image, following what the speech describes in words: sheet 0010 shows only the body gesture of raising the arm and looking at the wrist, with no screen; sheet 0011 shows the hand in motion with the screen reacting; sheet 0012 shows the confirmation of the gesture on the screen. The evolution from reading the time to controlling the watch without touching it is recorded frame by frame.
- Five sheets carry no product interface at all and sustain the theme through human image alone: an interviewee holding the dog's leash (sheet 0002, q0017) and a close-up of the guide dog being petted (q0018); a frame where the person leaves the scene and only the background, part of her body and the white cane on the left remain (sheet 0005, q0041); the gesture of looking at the wrist with no screen on sheet 0010; and pure testimonials on sheets 0009 and 0013.

Visual proportion: seven of the fourteen sheets show an Apple Watch screen (0003, 0004, 0006, 0007, 0008, 0011, 0012), five are testimonial only with no product (0002, 0005, 0009, 0010, 0013) and the remaining two are institutional opening and closing, with no slide or code demonstration anywhere in the video.

Recorded divergences or limits: on sheet 0010 the notes record that the watch on the interviewee's wrist is not identifiable as an Apple Watch and appears to be an ordinary analog watch, that is, the gesture of checking the time is shown as a body action, with no watch face detail. Sheet 0013 is recorded as interviewees only, with no screen at all.
