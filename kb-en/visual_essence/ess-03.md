# Visual essence, batch ess-03

Basis: 47 visual syntheses of HIG pages, from pointing-devices to web-views.

## Every page cover is the same construction drawing: a flat symbol in one color from the old six-color logo, over a gradient, with the dashed grid and the concentric circles left in view
- Evidence: printing (img 0889, red printer over an orange gradient with a rectangular mesh and a concentric circle on the body)
- Evidence: settings (img 1001, gear with the internal spokes aligned to the grid's diagonals)
- Evidence: searching (img 0994, magnifying glass ring concentric to the guide circle, handle coinciding with the diagonal)
- Evidence: siri (img 1079, blue ring with a smaller guide circumference marking the proportion of the inner stroke)
- Evidence: undo-and-redo (img 1205), remotes (img 0921), shazamkit (img 1027), wallet (img 1229), privacy (img 0890)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: Apple's mark of quality is not the final drawing, it is the mesh underneath it; every system symbol is born from a guide circle and shared axes, which makes pieces drawn by different people look like they belong to the same family.

## The opening of a component is a dimensioned spec sheet, not an app capture: the component appears isolated, with ruler arrows for width and height
- Evidence: pop-up-buttons (img 0883, arrows dimensioning the menu's width and the selected row's height)
- Evidence: steppers (img 1107, horizontal width bracket at the top and vertical height bracket on the right)
- Evidence: text-views (img 1143) and text-fields (img 1138, same construction, changing only the example content)
- Evidence: toggles (img 1147), tab-views (img 1118, dimensions on the container's four edges), web-views (img 1285), popovers (img 0886), sliders (img 1080)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: before it is content, the component is a box with declared dimensions; documenting the rectangle and its row height comes before documenting the behavior.

## Right and wrong come in pairs that change only one variable, and the judgment seal stays isolated in its own frame, never over the example

- What this teaches about building interfaces: to teach a rule, freeze everything and move one parameter; and keep the verdict outside the artifact, so the image keeps serving as a pixel reference.

- Evidence: status-bars (img 1105 and 1106, same photo and same framing, with and without the bars)

## Selection announces itself through a filled background behind the element, almost never through a border, underline or text color alone
- Evidence: segmented-controls (img 0996 and 0997, active segment in solid blue with a white glyph; img 0998 and 0999, active segment in white raised over gray)
- Evidence: tab-views (img 1118 and 1119, active tab marked by a more opaque background, and the dividers disappear beside it)

- Evidence: toggles (img 1150 and 1151, filter button communicates state through a fill behind the symbol, with no switch track), sidebars (img 1047), toolbars (img 1182)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: the active state needs area, not outline; a filled background survives a small screen, color blindness and an image background, something a thin underline does not do.

## The pill is the universal unit of grouping and of state
- Evidence: tab-bars (img 1108, four tabs inside a pill-shaped container, with search outside it)
- Evidence: toolbars (img 1163 and img 1176 to 1179, controls gathered into a single capsule or split into two capsules according to how related the actions are)
- Evidence: token-fields (img 1160 to 1162, a converted recipient becomes a pill visually distinct from loose text)
- Evidence: typography (img 1194, plant names get a translucent capsule to stay legible over the scenery), sliders (img 1086), steppers (img 1107), search-fields (img 0993)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: the capsule solves two things at once, it says what belongs together and it creates the contrast floor where the text would otherwise float; adopting it as the base shape reduces the number of border decisions.

## Search never shares the same container with navigation destinations
- Evidence: search-fields (img 0985 and 0986, first search is the fifth item inside the tab grouping, then it becomes a separate circular button, outside the group, at the far edge)
- Evidence: tab-bars (img 1108, the anatomy is born with the tab pill on one side and the circular search button on the other; img 1113, even in the minimized state search stays in an isolated button on the right)
- Evidence: search-fields (img 0988, in the top toolbar the magnifying glass groups with the more-options button, not with the add button)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: search is an action, not a place; mixing it with destinations makes the user read a verb as if it were a section.

## Anatomy is taught through a straight-line callout that leaves the element and ends in a label outside the drawing
- Evidence: status-bars (img 1104, each icon descends through a thin line to the label, with the Wi-Fi callout crossing beneath the cellular signal one)
- Evidence: typography (img 1198, real screenshot of Mail with Large title, Title, Subtitle and Body text pointing to the corresponding passages)
- Evidence: toolbars (img 1175, zone labels dividing the Freeform bar into three regions; img 1180, callouts naming the toolbar and the Finder window frame)
- Evidence: split-views (img 1099), tab-bars (img 1110), search-fields (img 0984), snippets (img 1091), wallet (img 1268)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: naming regions with a line and an external label keeps the capture intact and teaches vocabulary; it is the same habit that makes a design system have stable names for a bar's zones.

## A menu is a floating card with a shadow that covers part of the content behind it, and never a new screen
- Evidence: pop-up-buttons (img 0885, the menu emerges over the list itself and partially covers the neighboring rows)
- Evidence: pull-down-buttons (img 0917, white card with a shadow over the note, mixing a row of icons at the top with a vertical list below)
- Evidence: the-menu-bar (img 1145, on iPad the edit menu repeats the macOS structure as a light floating panel over the black screen)
- Evidence: token-fields (img 1161 and 1162), search-fields (img 0989 and 0990, suggestions right below the field with the first one highlighted)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: choosing a value is not changing place; keeping the origin visible anchors the decision and returns the user to context without navigation.

## A thin divider creates groups within the menu, and what is destructive or outside the pattern stays isolated at the end
- Evidence: pull-down-buttons (img 0917, destructive action last, separated and in red)
- Evidence: the-menu-bar (img 1144, edit menu in three groups, undo and redo, then cut, copy, paste and delete, and find last)
- Evidence: token-fields (img 1162, token menu in four blocks separated by dividers, from the current address to the search by name)
- Evidence: pop-up-buttons (img 0885, the custom option stays isolated at the end by a separator)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: hierarchy within a menu is built with spacing and order, not with bold; the last position is the most expensive to reach and that is why the irreversible goes there.

## Button hierarchy is fill weight, and the inactive state is the same button faded, not a different gray button
- Evidence: researchkit (img 0927, an asymmetric decision with "Disagree" as plain text and "Accept" as a solid yellow button; img 0929, the same yellow in a pale version while nothing has been selected)
- Evidence: sheets (img 1037 to 1039, a sequence of three frames where the checkmark goes from inactive gray to active blue and the X turns into a back arrow, a state machine taught through comparison)
- Evidence: snippets (img 1089, "Cancel" in light gray on the left and "Order" in blue and wider on the right), shazamkit (img 1028, "Not Now" gray and "Allow" blue), sign-in-with-apple (img 1054, "Create Account" neutral above Apple's black button)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: keeping the hue and changing the saturation preserves the button's identity between states; switching to generic gray makes the user look for a new control instead of recognizing the same one.

## The same component crosses platforms keeping its function and changing color, material and shape
- Evidence: progress-indicators (img 0907 and 0908, blue over light gray on macOS; img 0913 and 0914, white over dark gray inside a black frame that mimics the watch face on watchOS)
- Evidence: sliders (img 1082, blue track on a gray card on macOS; img 1086 and 1087, compact black capsule with a green fill and volume icons at the ends on watchOS)
- Evidence: sheets (img 1030 lower half on iPhone, img 1042 centered card over a dimmed window on macOS, img 1043 full screen on watchOS, video 053 growing from the center on visionOS)
- Evidence: toolbars (img 1163, 1181, 1183), tab-bars (img 1109, 1114, 1116), search-fields (img 0983, 0990, 0993)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: what is portable is the component's role and its position in the flow; color, material and entry point are local properties of the platform and should be treated as tokens, not as part of the definition.

## On watchOS, navigation between pages becomes a column of marks next to the Digital Crown, with no text or arrows
- Evidence: split-views (img 1103, thin column of marks with a longer, lighter segment highlighted, beside a list over a black background)
- Evidence: tab-views (img 1120, current dot larger and white in a vertical column, being the screen's only navigation element)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: when the screen cannot fit a control, the indicator migrates to the edge and leans on the hardware; the physical control's position becomes part of the visual grammar.

## Layers resolve through translucency and blur, with the background continuing behind instead of being cut off
- Evidence: sidebars (img 1048 with X, a flower image stopping at the sidebar edge with no transition; img 1050 with check, the same image continuing blurred and mirrored behind the items to the window edge)
- Evidence: scroll-views (img 0981, hard edge with opaque blur ending in a defined line; img 0982, soft edge dissolving into the content and leaving the title and buttons almost floating)
- Evidence: toolbars (img 1181 and 1182, translucent bar in visionOS with the blurred environment visible through the material), tab-bars (img 1116 and 1117), snippets (img 1089 and 1090, home screen blurred behind the card), sheets (video 053, parent window losing sharpness after the sheet appears)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: the junction between layers is where an interface looks cheap or expensive; resolving it through dissolution instead of a cut costs one line of CSS and changes the perception of the whole piece.

## Red horizontal guides for cap height and baseline measure the relationship between text and any neighboring element
- Evidence: sf-symbols (img 1015 to 1017, the same plus symbol next to the word "Add" in small, medium and large, with the circle sitting below, slightly above and clearly above the two guides)
- Evidence: typography (img 1195 and 1196, SF Pro and New York compared with the same pangram between two blue lines for cap height and baseline)
- Evidence: right-to-left (img 0953 and 0954, three buttons with the same label in Latin, Arabic and Hebrew; at the same body size only the Latin touches both guides, and with the Arabic and Hebrew font slightly larger the visual weight balances out)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: optical alignment is proven with two drawn lines, not with opinion; and equalizing the numeric size value across different scripts produces imbalance, not consistency.

## Safe zones and reserved areas are drawn as concentric rectangles or solid blocks over the finished art
- Evidence: top-shelf (img 1188 to 1190, three concentric rectangles with rulers on the right for actual size, safe zone in focus and size out of focus, the same scheme repeated at 2:3, 1:1 and 16:9)
- Evidence: wallet (img 1261 to 1264, the same illustration appears finished and then covered by blue blocks marking the header at the top and the code area and footer at the bottom, in a T shape when the code is a QR)
- Evidence: spatial-layout (img 1097, field of view as three circles of 30, 60 and 90 degrees over the room render, with the app window almost entirely inside the 60 circle), sf-symbols (img 1002)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: whoever delivers art needs to see the crop, not read about it; overlaying the mask on the real piece is the fastest way to communicate bleed and safety margin.

## Systematic variation is presented in a matrix: one axis per parameter and the same shape repeated in every cell
- Evidence: sf-symbols (img 1014, 27 samples of the same folder-plus symbol, nine columns from Ultralight to Black by three rows of scale; img 1018, a two-by-five grid with outline on top and fill below; img 1019, eight rows by eleven columns changing only the internal character by script)
- Evidence: typography (img 1197, the word "Text" in nine weights by two rows, roman and italic, with no interface around it)
- Evidence: virtual-keyboards (img 1207 to 1218, each keyboard type in the same composition, name in gray at the top, field with placeholder and keyboard aligned to the base, which allows key-by-key comparison)
- Evidence: wallet (img 1278 to 1283, the same three-line skeleton repeated by pass style, changing the use of the lines but never the count), toggles (img 1153 to 1158)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: the matrix reveals the coverage gap that the chosen sample hides; if a parameter deserves to exist, it deserves its own row or column.

## Apple shows what happens when space runs out, and treats degradation as design, not as accident
- Evidence: toolbars (img 1164 and 1165, when narrowing the Notes window few icons remain and an overflow button with a double arrow appears, whose menu collapses even the More button itself)
- Evidence: typography (img 1199 and 1200, the same Mail message at the default size and at the largest accessibility size, with the name breaking into two lines, the body cut at the start and the footer icons overlapping the text)
- Evidence: text-fields (img 1140 to 1142, the same sentence cut at the edge with no ellipsis, broken into two lines and truncated with an ellipsis)
- Evidence: tab-bars (img 1112 and 1113, bar with an expanded accessory and then minimized into a circular button plus pill), virtual-keyboards (img 1219 to 1223, the Sign In button disappears behind the keyboard and then appears cut in half by the suggestion bar)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: every component needs a declared rule for what disappears first; without it, the decision falls to the browser, and what disappears is precisely the action button.

## Indeterminate is distinguished from determinate by the absence of a visible end, and the gray spinner always appears together with status text
- Evidence: progress-indicators (img 0912, indeterminate bar fully filled with a strong gradient at the center and almost transparent at the ends, suggesting a wave instead of progress; img 0911, gray spinner below the Mail status bar with the text "Updated Just Now")
- Evidence: tap-to-pay-on-iphone (img 1126 partial bar during preparation, img 1127 full bar treated as indeterminate, img 1132 the bar gives way to a gray spinner next to the label "Authorizing")
- Evidence: wallet (img 1265 and 1266, partial green bar when the order is placed, which disappears and turns into a green check when delivered)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: waiting with no prediction needs its own shape and its own caption; reusing the determinate bar for the indeterminate case promises an end that the system cannot deliver.

## In the videos the effect is isolated over a neutral scene, the elements animate out of phase with each other, and the text label only enters after the effect has already been seen
- Evidence: sf-symbols (video 042, the three symbols appear staggered from an empty frame to all three present; video 045, each symbol shrinks and returns out of phase with the others; video 046, only one layer of each symbol pulses at a time; video 051, one symbol breathes at a time in rotation across four sheets)
- Evidence: spatial-layout (videos 057 and 058, same gray test scene with a checkered floor, a hollow yellow outline as reference against the filled real window, and the captions "Dynamic Scale" and "Fixed Scale" entering only in the second half)
- Evidence: tab-bars (video 059, the bar goes from six icons with no text to the labels written out and back to collapsed, always in the same framing), sheets (video 053, four frames showing only growth from the center)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: simultaneous animation across all elements reads as a rendering failure; the phase offset is what conveys that the system is processing item by item, and the caption after the effect keeps the text from replacing observation.

## Modal always exposes the exit, and the only deliberate exception is the screen that precedes a permission request
- Evidence: sheets (img 1032 with X, top with grabber, title and only the blue confirmation button; img 1034 with check, gray X to cancel on the left, title at the center and confirmation on the right; img 1036, combining back, X and check at the same time is the pattern to avoid)
- Evidence: tap-to-pay-on-iphone (img 1125, tutorial in a modal sheet with a gray circular X in the top right corner), researchkit (img 0929 and 0930, white "x" to close in the top left corner on the survey and task screens)
- Evidence: privacy (img 0898 and 0899, the same pre-permission screen is marked as a fail exactly for gaining a "Cancel" stacked under the "Next" or an X in the top left corner)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: the exit is mandatory in a common modal because the cost is reversible; in the antechamber of a system alert the duplicated exit hides the real alert, so the rule inverts on purpose, and it is worth knowing which of the two cases you are building.

## A system button has a frozen design, and the only variable allowed is the text
- Evidence: sign-in-with-apple (img 1055 to 1057, the three titles on the same black button with logo and white text; img 1068 to 1070, the corner radius is a controlled progression from square corners to a capsule shape; img 1076 to 1078, the logo-only button appears under three masks)
- Evidence: tap-to-pay-on-iphone (img 1128 to 1131, the same full-width blue shape carries a check with the waves symbol and an X with the Apple logo in its place; img 1136 and 1137, the same shape with the label swapped)
- Evidence: wallet (img 1272 to 1275, four identity verification buttons with a single design, rounded black background and icon on the left, changing only the first line of text)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: when the shape is the brand, customization needs to be a declared and limited axis; offering radius, mask and label and locking the rest is safer than publishing a recommendation in prose.

## Icon at the leading edge and text after it, in every list row and menu item
- Evidence: the-menu-bar (img 1144, every edit menu item has an icon at the leading edge and a keyboard shortcut on the right)
- Evidence: sidebars (img 1047, folder icon on the left, label in the middle and star on the right, with vertical dashed guides aligning the two icon columns across the three rows)
- Evidence: pull-down-buttons (img 0917, vertical list with an icon on the left and some items with a submenu arrow and secondary text)
- Evidence: researchkit (img 0927 and 0932), privacy (img 0891, two-column grid with icon to the left of the text), wallet (img 1234)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: the icon column needs to be a real column, aligned across every row; it is the alignment, not the icon design, that makes the list look organized.

## Star rating is always a row of five, and the difference between full and empty is a shade of the same color, not a separate outline
- Evidence: rating-indicators (img 0918, three full stars in solid dark red and two empty in translucent light red, with no fractional star, with dimensions for the width of the row and the height of one star)
- Evidence: ratings-and-reviews (img 0919, cover with a single star split in half, solid on the left and outlined on the right; img 0920, the five stars of the rating request appear in blue outline and all empty, with no pre-selected rating)
- Evidence: right-to-left (img 0948 to 0950, three full, one half and one empty, with the numeral centered under each star and the count reversing direction in the RTL versions)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: state is communicated within the same color family, which keeps the row legible from a distance; and the input control starts out empty, because a pre-marked rating turns into a suggestion.

## Label outside the box on the left, value on the right, and the exact number lives in a field while the continuous control delivers only the approximation
- Evidence: sliders (img 1081, label "Opacity", slider with thumb, field with the percentage and stepper attached to the field on the same line, the thumb giving the position and the field giving the number)
- Evidence: text-fields (img 1139, numeric fields stacked with the label outside the box ending in a colon and the value aligned to the right inside the field)
- Evidence: pop-up-buttons (img 0884, grouped list with labels on the left and values on the right), researchkit (img 0931, profile as label and value pairs with a navigation arrow)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: continuous control and numeric field don't compete, they complement each other; whoever needs precision types, whoever needs speed drags, and the label outside the box keeps the column of values aligned.

## System alert changes layout according to the number of choices: two buttons side by side, three stacked
- Evidence: privacy (img 0894 and 0895, location and photos with three stacked buttons, and the location one also adds a map preview with a "Precise: On" badge; img 0896, contacts with only two buttons side by side and "Allow" highlighted in blue)
- Evidence: shazamkit (img 1028, microphone alert with "Not Now" in gray on the left and "Allow" in blue on the right, side by side)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: the count of options determines the shape of the action block; two choices fit on one line and read as a pair, three need a stack so the order of preference stays clear.

## On almost half the pages the image carries information that the official caption doesn't describe, and sometimes contradicts it
- Evidence: web-views (the caption speaks only of a tinted compass, and img 1285 also brings the rectangle that frames it and the width and height arrows)
- Evidence: watch-faces (the description speaks of a stylized representation of watch faces in red, and img 1284 brings three watch faces named in full, linked by a connecting line, over a gradient from orange to pink)
- Evidence: tab-bars (video 059 shows the faint background circle on the "Memories" icon also in the collapsed state, at q001, q002 and q010, which the description doesn't mention), sheets (img 1029 adds measurement arrows the text doesn't detail; in video 053 the background is a recognizable living room, not the blank window described), sf-symbols (in video 051 only the opacity variation is perceptible, not the growing and shrinking that the description narrates)
- What this teaches about building interfaces: caption and image age at different rates; whoever uses the HIG as a source needs to measure in the image and treat the text as a summary, not as a specification.

## Single-source findings

- Touch target margin with a number: a blue button with a bezel gets 12 of clearance on all four sides, while a small information symbol and a borderless button, with only blue text, get 24, that is, double the margin for the element that offers less surface. Reference: pointing-devices (img 0861 to 0863).
- Dimensioned proportion of the custom sign-in button: 44 pt height with 19 pt font and 56 pt with 24 pt font, a ratio between height and text body close to 2.3 in both cases. Reference: sign-in-with-apple (img 1074 and 1075).
- Height ceiling of the customizable area of a snippet, annotated as a vertical dimension in the page's only numeric diagram: 400 pt for the custom view between the dialog block at the top and the pair of buttons at the bottom. Reference: snippets (img 1091).
- Product image specified by a 300 px ruler on both edges, over a solid background. Reference: wallet (img 1269).
- In the Top Shelf banner with scrolling, the order of the labels reverses relative to the fixed-proportion formats: the safe zone in focus now points to the outermost edge and the actual size to the middle line. Reference: top-shelf (img 1191).
- The person's posture changes the panel's orientation, not just its height: for someone sitting upright the panel is born vertical, and for someone reclined in an armchair it is born elevated and tilted toward the face. Reference: spatial-layout (videos 054 and 055, sheet 0001, q001 to q006).
- Dynamic scale against fixed scale, taught with the same device: a hollow yellow outline marks the reference size while the actual window grows or shrinks, and the yellow line connecting the two goes from diagonal to vertical at the end, suggesting that the scene turns to present itself frontally again. Reference: spatial-layout (videos 057 and 058, sheets 0001 and 0002).
- The stepper is the only control in the set whose behavior is annotated in pseudocode, with "i++" connected by a line to the upper half and "i--" to the lower half. Reference: steppers (img 1107).
- Context keys occupy part of the space bar without changing the base QWERTY: "@" and "." on the email keyboard, "@" and "#" on the social network one, and ".", "/" and ".com" before the return key on the URL one; and the search keyboard swaps the gray return key for a blue key with an arrow. Reference: virtual-keyboards (img 1211, 1216, 1217, 1218).
- The Shift state is communicated by the fill of the glyph, not by background color: on the name and phone keyboard the letters appear in lowercase and the Shift key has a hollow outline. Reference: virtual-keyboards (img 1212 compared to img 1210).
- The mixed state of the checkbox is the same blue square with a white horizontal stroke in place of the check, not a third design; checkbox and radio share the vocabulary of a blue fill with a distinct internal white mark. Reference: toggles (img 1153, 1155, 1157).
- Radio buttons in a horizontal row receive blocks of the same width even with labels of very different sizes. Reference: toggles (img 1159).
- The number keeps the internal order of digits in Latin, Hebrew and Arabic, in the same body size and weight as the surrounding text, including in the variant with Eastern Arabic numerals. Reference: right-to-left (img 0944 to 0947).
- Mirroring in RTL is decided per component, not per screen: the alignment guide and the text flip to the side while the central image icon stays in the same place, and in a composite icon the plus badge follows the direction of the cart only in the full RTL variant. Reference: right-to-left (img 0934 and 0935; img 0975 to 0977).
- Symbols that contain real text are localized, not just mirrored: signature, document with lettering and large letter with cursor appear in Latin, Hebrew and Arabic, with the character migrating to the opposite corner. Reference: right-to-left (img 0963 to 0965).
- The tab bar badge is a red circle attached to the icon's upper right corner, and appears without a number in the instance shown. Reference: tab-bars (img 1111).
- The attached popover loses the point that connected it to its origin when it's detached, and only then does it gain a title bar with an X to close; the internal content stays identical. Reference: popovers (img 0887 and 0888).
- When hiding the interface in full-screen media, the status bar and the navigation bar disappear together, as a single layer. Reference: status-bars (img 1105 and 1106).
- The VoiceOver grouping border defines what is read as a unit: enclosing two photos and their two captions is disapproved, enclosing one photo with its caption is approved. Reference: voiceover (img 1225 to 1228).
- Dialog text that repeats name, date and time already visible on the internal card is disapproved; the approved example eliminates the dialog and leaves the information only inside the view. Reference: snippets (img 1092 to 1095).
- In the anteroom of a permission, besides the exit, also prohibited are a financial incentive (dollar sign in a purple circle with a credit offer), a chart of growing benefit and an annotated reproduction of the real system alert with the desired button circled or pointed to by an arrow. Reference: privacy (img 0900 to 0903).
- Research consent uses a green check per item next to the legal paragraph and the typed signature, with the final decision deliberately asymmetric, "Disagree" as plain text and "Accept" as a solid button. Reference: researchkit (img 0927).
- Progress in a long flow appears written and drawn at the same time, with the count in text ("Step 1 of 5") next to a bar in the corresponding position. Reference: researchkit (img 0925, 0926, 0929).
- SharePlay's three spatial templates communicate the difference only through the relative position between people and content, with no text, ruler or annotation: lined up facing a panel, in a circle around a sphere, or in a closed half circle with the panel pushed to the edge. Reference: shareplay (img 1024 to 1026).
- In the alignment segmented control, a thin vertical divider line separates the last icon from the first three, suggesting a subgroup within the same control. Reference: segmented-controls (img 0996).
- Tick marks can cover the whole track and not just the filled part, and not every mark gets a label: in the Energy Saver panel only some points on the scale are named. Reference: sliders (img 1083 and 1085).
- An extra menu bar has a more compact menu with no keyboard shortcuts next to the items, unlike the menus in the main bar. Reference: the-menu-bar (img 1146).
- In the Wallet coupon, the primary field sits overlapping the illustrated strip at the top, not beside it, something only the diagram with dashed rectangles makes clear. Reference: wallet (img 1243 and 1258).
- The Pass Designer on Mac organizes editing in a side tree by field type, header, primary, footer and back, with the preview on the right and a minimum iOS version label above it. Reference: wallet (img 1235).
- The pointer in iPadOS is a gray circle that darkens and grows as it approaches the button and lightens and shrinks as it moves away, while the button's own fill doesn't change color. Reference: pointing-devices (video 038, sheet 0001, q001 to q007).
- The selection of an object is drawn with white square handles at the corners and at the midpoints, a central handle with arrows, and a dark label beside it showing width and height in points. Reference: pointing-devices (img 0864).
